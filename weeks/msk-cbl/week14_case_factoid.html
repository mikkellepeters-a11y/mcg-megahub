Week 14 CBL — Low Back Pain Factoid

MSK–Skin · Mr. O’Connell · Condensed high-yield case review
CASE ANCHOR: L2–L3 paracentral disc herniation → compression of the traversing L3 root → new radicular syndrome. Rule: paracentral = traversing; far lateral = exiting.

Common mechanical causes

  • Lumbar strain: most common acute adult LBP; after activity/injury; paraspinal pain, movement-related.
  • Disc herniation: acute/subacute ± precipitating event; worse with sitting/hip flexion; radicular features.
  • Degenerative arthritis: gradual, age-associated; osteophytes can cause radiculopathy.
  • Spondylolisthesis: anterior vertebral displacement; back/posterior thigh ache worse with activity/bending.

Stenosis vs inflammatory pain

  • Spinal stenosis: canal narrowing; leg pain with walking/standing (neurogenic claudication); relieved by sitting.
  • Ankylosing spondylitis: often ages 15–40; morning stiffness, SI/lumbar aching; improves with exercise.
  • SI joint: low back/buttock pain from sacroiliac joint pathology.

Cauda equina — emergency

  • Large mass effect compressing cauda equina (disc, tumor or epidural abscess).
  • Urinary retention, saddle anesthesia, bowel dysfunction, weakness → urgent MRI and immediate specialist evaluation for decompression.
  • New bowel/bladder symptoms distinguish from uncomplicated radicular pain.

Infection, fracture, malignancy

  • Discitis/osteomyelitis: constant focal pain, spinous tenderness; ESR/CRP often high; fever/CBC may be normal.
  • Fracture: trauma or osteoporosis/steroids; focal bony tenderness → radiographs.
  • Malignancy: cancer history, age >50, weight loss, persistent/progressive pain often worse recumbent.
  • Spinal epidural abscess: important infectious cause of neurologic compression.

Nonspinal mimics

  • Renal stone: abrupt severe flank/LBP → groin; hematuria may occur.
  • Pyelonephritis: fever + urinary symptoms commonly accompany back/flank pain.
  • AAA: older age, hypertension, smoking; abdominal/back/flank pain; can mimic stone.
  • Visceral referral: pelvic/GI disease, prostatitis, pancreatitis; abdominal/pelvic symptoms guide workup.

Disc anatomy & pathogenesis

  • Nucleus pulposus: gelatinous center; annulus fibrosus: fibrocartilage ring.
  • Annular damage permits nucleus material to herniate and impinge the nerve root.
  • Degeneration: ↓ nucleus hydration, ↑ type I collagen, ECM breakdown, apoptosis, MMPs + inflammation.

Disc herniation symptom pattern

  • Radicular pain, sensory change and weakness in one or more root distributions.
  • Reduced trunk flexion; worsened by sitting, cough, sneeze or straining.
  • Distribution depends on disc level + herniation position, not disc level alone.

Workup clues

  • Cauda equina / major progressive deficit: urgent MRI.
  • Suspected vertebral infection: MRI, CBC, ESR, CRP.
  • Suspected compression fracture: plain radiographs.
  • Suspected inflammatory spondylitis: pelvis imaging and inflammatory testing as indicated.
  • Suspected malignancy: MRI and additional evaluation as indicated.
