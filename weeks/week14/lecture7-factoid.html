Shoulder & Posterior Arm — Factoid Sheet

Week 14 · Lecture 7 · Dr. Hannah · Condensed Week 13-style review
3 anchors: GH = mobile but unstable (labrum + cuff); 2:1 GH/scapular rhythm; shaft = radial/profunda brachii vs surgical neck = axillary/post circumflex humeral.

Osteology & fractures

  • Clavicle weakest at mid / middle–lateral junction; SCM pulls medial segment up; shoulder drops.
  • Surgical neck → axillary n. + posterior circumflex humeral a.
  • Radial groove / shaft → radial n. + profunda brachii a.
  • Supraglenoid = long-head biceps; infraglenoid = long-head triceps.

SC vs AC vs GH

  • SC saddle, only bony upper limb–trunk bridge; interclavicular / sternoclavicular / costoclavicular ligaments.
  • AC plane; coracoclavicular = conoid + trapezoid, primary stabilizer; shoulder separation.
  • GH ball-and-socket; ~30% humeral head contact; anterior dislocation ~90%; check axillary neurovascular exam.

GH stabilizers

  • Glenoid labrum: deepens socket ~50%; superior labrum linked to long-head biceps.
  • Capsule is lax; GH ligament complex, coracohumeral ligament and coracoacromial arch resist displacement.
  • Rotator cuff stabilizes through humeral head compression / inferior control.

Rotator cuff SITS

  • Supraspinatus: greater tubercle superior; abducts; suprascapular.
  • Infraspinatus: greater tubercle middle; external rotation; suprascapular.
  • Teres minor: greater tubercle inferior; external rotation; axillary.
  • Subscapularis: lesser tubercle; internal rotation; upper/lower subscapular.

Deltoid / teres major

  • Deltoid: lateral clavicle/acromion/spine → deltoid tuberosity; anterior flex+IR, middle abduct, posterior extend+ER; axillary nerve.
  • Teres major: inferior scapular angle → medial lip bicipital groove; adduct+IR; lower subscapular n.; not cuff.

Scapulohumeral rhythm

  • Approximate GH:scapula = 2:1 during elevation.
  • Full 180° = ~120° GH + 60° upward scapular rotation; variable.
  • Supraspinatus tear is most common cuff tear; overhead use; trouble initiating abduction.

Posterior arm

  • Triceps heads: long infraglenoid tubercle; lateral above radial groove; medial below.
  • All → olecranon; extension of elbow; radial nerve.
  • Deep brachial artery accompanies radial nerve in groove.

Nerve clues

  • Axillary: deltoid + teres minor, lateral shoulder skin; flattened contour, loss abduction.
  • Suprascapular: supraspinatus/infraspinatus.
  • Army over/Navy under superior transverse scapular ligament (artery over / nerve under).

Three posterior spaces

  • Quadrangular: teres minor/major + triceps long head + humerus → axillary n. + post circumflex humeral a.
  • Triangular space: teres minor/major + long triceps → circumflex scapular a.
  • Triangular interval: teres major + long triceps + humerus → radial n. + profunda brachii a.

Vascular connections

  • Scapular anastomoses: dorsal scapular ↔ suprascapular ↔ circumflex scapular.
  • Thoracoacromial branches = clavicular, acromial, deltoid, pectoral.
  • Subscapular → thoracodorsal + circumflex scapular; posterior circumflex humeral accompanies axillary nerve.

Clinical distinctions

  • AC separation = ligament injury at lateral clavicle vs GH dislocation = humeral head displacement.
  • Adhesive capsulitis: fibrotic GH capsule, stiffness; diabetes/immobilization risk.
  • Imaging: lecture shows clavicle, shaft and surgical-neck fracture X-rays plus supraspinatus tear MRI.
