Physical Therapy for Low Back Pain — Factoid

Ashley Fox, PT, DPT · MSK–Skin · Condensed Week 13-style study sheet
TRIAGE FIRST: red flags → medical; yellow flags → psychosocial-informed rehab; low risk → education/activity/self-care as appropriate. Stable radicular pain ≠ progressive neurological deficit.

Definitions & framing

  • LBP: below costal margin and above inferior gluteal folds ± leg pain.
  • Acute <3 months; chronic >3 months; recurrent = repeat acute episodes.

PT examination

  • History + exam + biopsychosocial context.
  • Movement patterns, ROM, strength, posture/ergonomics, segmental mobility.
  • Minimal emphasis on unverified pathoanatomic labels in nonspecific LBP.

Red flags → medical

  • TUNA FISH on slide: trauma, unexplained weight loss, neurologic findings, age >50, fever, IV drug use, steroid use, cancer history.
  • Progressive neurologic deficits, serious spinal disease, or illness precluding rehab: medical management.
  • Stable leg pain/radiculopathy is not automatically a red flag.

Yellow flags → psychosocial

  • Fear-avoidance, pain catastrophizing, depressed mood, anxiety, low support, social withdrawal, beliefs activity is harmful.
  • Affect risk of prolonged disability; prompt tailored education/PT ± mental-health referral.

Yellow-flag tools

  • PHQ-9 mood; TSK kinesiophobia; FABQ fear-avoidance.
  • PCS catastrophizing; PASS-20 pain anxiety.
  • STarT Back, OSPRO-YF, Örebro = broader risk screening.

3 management lanes

  • Medical: red flags, significant medical limits, progressive neuro deficits.
  • Rehab: medium/high psychosocial risk or predominantly leg pain; controlled/minor comorbidity.
  • Self-care: low risk, mainly back pain, mild/controlled comorbidity.

Acute advice

  • Reassuring education + favorable prognosis; keep moving as tolerated.
  • No prolonged bed rest; counsel with nonthreatening language.
  • Consider PT referral after screening for red flags.

Chronic advice

  • Persistent pain can reflect neuroplastic / contextual factors; not proof of persistent tissue injury.
  • Graded movement, education, PT and psychosocial support where needed.

Imaging trap

  • Degenerative changes common in asymptomatic adults and rise with age.
  • Disk degeneration: 37% at 20, 68% at 40, 88% at 60, 96% at 80.
  • A scan finding is not automatically a pain source.

Interventions & case

  • Education, activity, manual therapy, specific exercise.
  • 52F, 1 week right gluteal/back pain + thigh referral, normal neuro testing, no red flags: active care / PT, not bed rest or emergent MRI.

Fast differentiators

ClueBest response
Progressive neurological lossMedical management, not routine PT-only pathway
High fear-avoidance or depressed moodScreen yellow flags, targeted counseling, PT ± further referral
Age-related disc degeneration without concordanceDo not label imaging as sole pain generator
1-week pain, normal neuro exam, no red flagsReassure, stay active, consider PT
