Pectoral Region & Axilla — Factoid

MSK-Skin · Week 14 · Lecture 1 · Manuel · Condensed Week 13-style main-lecture review
Anchor set: Breast lymph mostly axillary; nipple T4 · Pec minor divides axillary a. into 1–2–3 branches · Plexus C5–T1, cords around artery · Long thoracic → winged scapula · Erb waiter’s tip vs Klumpke claw.

Bones & key joints

  • Sternal angle = 2nd rib / T4–T5.
  • Clavicle: SC to AC joint; shelters neurovascular bundle.
  • Scapula coracoid = pec minor anchor; acromion = AC joint; glenoid = GH joint.

Breast: structural map

  • Typical ribs 2–6; axillary tail of Spence.
  • Alveoli → lobules → lactiferous ducts/sinuses → nipple.
  • Cooper ligaments tether skin; retromammary space allows mobility.
  • Nipple dermatome T4.

Breast blood & lymph

  • Medial: internal thoracic; lateral: lateral thoracic; intercostals 2–6.
  • >75% lymph → axillary nodes; medial → parasternal; inferior → abdominal.
  • Sentinel node = first draining node; surgery/radiation → arm lymphedema.

Breast clinical correlations

  • Dimpling: Cooper ligament traction.
  • Peau d’orange: dermal lymphatic obstruction.
  • Nipple retraction: duct fibrosis/traction.

Pectoral muscle quartet

  • Pec major: medial clavicle/sternum/ribs → lateral lip intertubercular groove; flex/add/medially rotate; both pectoral nerves.
  • Pec minor: ribs 3–5 → coracoid; stabilizes scapula; medial pectoral n.
  • Subclavius: rib 1 → clavicle; depresses; nerve to subclavius.
  • Serratus: ribs 1–8 → medial scapula; protract/upward rotate; long thoracic C5–7.

Winged scapula

  • Long thoracic C5–C7 injury → weak serratus anterior; medial scapula lifts from thorax on wall push.
  • Subscapularis → lesser tubercle, medial rotation, upper/lower subscapular nn.

Axilla: boundaries

  • Apex = clavicle + 1st rib + superior scapula.
  • Anterior = pec major/minor; medial = serratus/thoracic wall.
  • Posterior = subscapularis, lat dorsi, teres major; lateral = humeral groove.
  • Contents = axillary artery/vein, plexus, nodes, fat.

Axillary artery: 1–2–3

  • Lateral rib 1 → axillary; inferior teres major → brachial.
  • Part 1: superior thoracic.
  • Part 2: thoracoacromial (clavicular, acromial, deltoid, pectoral) + lateral thoracic.
  • Part 3: subscapular (circumflex scapular + thoracodorsal), ant/post circumflex humeral.

Veins

  • Axillary = brachial + basilic; lies anterior to artery; becomes subclavian at lateral rib 1.
  • Cephalic → deltopectoral groove → axillary vein near clavicle.

Plexus assembly

  • C5–T1 anterior rami → Roots, Trunks, Divisions, Cords, Branches.
  • Trunks: C5–6 upper, C7 middle, C8–T1 lower.
  • Cords relative to axillary artery: lateral = anterior upper+middle; medial = anterior lower; posterior = all posterior divisions.

Plexus branches

  • Roots: dorsal scapular C5; long thoracic C5–7.
  • Upper trunk: suprascapular, nerve to subclavius.
  • Lateral cord: lateral pectoral; musculocutaneous; lateral median root.
  • Medial cord: medial pectoral, medial cutaneous arm/forearm, ulnar + medial median root.
  • Posterior cord: upper/lower subscapular, thoracodorsal, axillary, radial.

Terminal nerves

  • Musculocutaneous C5–7 → arm flexors + lateral forearm.
  • Median C5–T1 → most forearm flexors + lateral 3½ digits.
  • Ulnar C8–T1 → hand intrinsics + medial 1½ digits.
  • Axillary C5–6 → deltoid/teres minor + lateral shoulder.
  • Radial C5–T1 → extensors + posterior arm/forearm.

Erb vs Klumpke

  • Erb C5–6: waiter’s tip; poor shoulder abduction/external rotation, elbow flexion/supination.
  • Klumpke C8–T1: intrinsic hand loss → claw hand + medial sensation loss.
