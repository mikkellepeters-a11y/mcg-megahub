Non-Neoplastic Bone Pathology

Inherited defects · Acquired bone disorders · Histology + laboratory differentiation

Master distinction: Osteoporosis = too little normally mineralized bone · Osteomalacia = inadequately mineralized osteoid · Paget = excess, disorganized remodeling.

Inherited disorders · one-step recognition

DisorderDefect → consequenceRecognize it
AchondroplasiaActivating FGFR3 (AD, often de novo) → ↓ growth plate chondrocyte proliferation → ↓ endochondral growth. Intramembranous growth relatively preserved.Rhizomelic shortening; relatively normal trunk, frontal bossing, macrocephaly, midface hypoplasia, trident hands. Possible spinal stenosis.
Osteogenesis imperfectaCOL1A1/COL1A2 → abnormal or reduced type I collagen → fragile osteoid matrix (often AD).Repeated fractures + blue/gray sclerae; dentinogenesis imperfecta, hearing loss, wormian bones, ligamentous laxity.
OsteopetrosisOsteoclast resorption failure → retained primary bone and calcified cartilage → dense but brittle skeleton; narrowed marrow cavities.Osteosclerosis + fractures; bone-within-bone, sandwich vertebrae; anemia/thrombocytopenia, extramedullary hematopoiesis, cranial nerve compression.

Acquired disorders · core comparison

DiseaseProcess / histologyTypical labsClinical + imaging clue
OsteoporosisResorption > formation → thin trabeculae / cortex; remaining osteoid properly mineralized.Usually normal routine bone labs; DXA T-score ≤ −2.5 in applicable adults.Low-trauma vertebral, hip, distal radius fractures; low bone density/compression.
OsteomalaciaDefective mineralization → excess unmineralized osteoid; vitamin D or phosphate deficiency / wasting.↑ ALP, often ↓ phosphate, ↓ 25-OH D with D deficiency; Ca low/normal, PTH may ↑.Diffuse bone pain, proximal weakness, gait difficulty; Looser zones (pseudofractures).
Paget diseaseFocal ↑ osteoclast activity → exuberant formation; mosaic lamellar bone + irregular cement lines.↑ ALP; Ca and phosphate usually normal.Enlarged/thickened bone, deformity, hearing loss; mixed lytic/sclerotic changes.
ALP reflects osteoblast activity, not a specific diagnosis. Osteomalacia in children with growth-plate involvement = rickets.

Don’t mix these up · exam traps

MATRIX OI ≠ osteomalacia: defective collagen vs defective mineral deposition.
DENSITY Osteopetrosis ≠ osteoporosis: high-density brittle bone vs low-density fragile bone.
GROWTH Achondroplasia: impaired endochondral ossification; not a collagen defect.
HISTO Thin trabeculae = osteoporosis; wide osteoid seams = osteomalacia.
HISTO Mosaic / jigsaw cement lines = Paget disease.
BOARD High ALP + normal Ca/PO₄ + enlarging skull = Paget, not osteoporosis.

Lecture → Step 1 · selective connections

FGFR3 gain of function → shortened proximal extremities · Type I collagen is abundant in bone, sclera, dentin → multisystem OI clues · Osteoclast failure → marrow failure despite dense bone · Vitamin D deficiency → mineralization failure, secondary ↑ PTH possible · Paget → uncommon secondary osteosarcoma; extensive disease rarely causes high-output failure.

Rapid-fire recall · answer in a phrase

1. FGFR3 activation affects which bone process? Endochondral growth
2. Blue sclerae + fractures? Osteogenesis imperfecta
3. Dense bone + marrow failure? Osteopetrosis
4. Normal mineralization but reduced mass? Osteoporosis
5. Looser zones + proximal weakness? Osteomalacia
6. Jigsaw cement lines + ↑ ALP? Paget disease
