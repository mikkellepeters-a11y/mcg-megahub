Core numbers, cells & mechanisms

Bone wet weight

60% hydroxyapatite • 30% organic • 10% water

Organic matrix

90% type I collagen; 10% ground substance + noncollagenous proteins

Adult skeleton

80% cortical / 20% trabecular by mass

Surface-to-volume

Cortical ~2.5 / trabecular ~20

Osteoclast signals

RANKL + M-CSF ↑; OPG ↓

Osteoblast brake

Sclerostin, Dkk1 ↓ formation

Cortical / trabecular BMUTunnel generating osteon / surface resorption and refilling

Resorption > formation → bone loss (e.g. menopause, aging). Anti-resorptives: bisphosphonates, SERMs, denosumab. Anabolics: teriparatide, abaloparatide, romosozumab.

Markers: osteoclast = collagen telopeptides, crosslinks, TRAP; osteoblast = collagen propeptides, osteocalcin, ALP.

Cartilage comparison

TypeVisible histologyWhere / special note
HyalineGlassy, basophilic; type II fibrils invisible at LMJoint surfaces, trachea, costal cartilage, growth plate
ElasticVisible elastic fiber network; chondrocytes numerousAuricle, epiglottis; bend/recoil
FibrocartilageRows of chondrocytes amid thick type I collagenMeniscus, IV disc, pubic symphysis; no perichondrium

Aggrecan + hyaluronan + negatively charged GAGs → attracts water → compression resistance. Appositional growth via inner perichondrial chondroblasts; interstitial growth via dividing chondrocytes and isogenic groups. No perichondrium: articular cartilage, growth plate cartilage, fibrocartilage.

First Aid rapid connections

  1. Type I collagen defect → OI → recurrent fractures plus blue sclerae.
  2. Insufficient hydroxyapatite deposition → osteomalacia → increased osteoid.
  3. Focal disordered remodeling → Paget → mosaic bone / alkaline phosphatase.
  4. RANKL promotes osteoclasts → denosumab targets RANKL.
  5. Articular cartilage degradation → OA → narrowing/osteophytes/subchondral sclerosis.
