Bone Growth, Formation, & Fractures

Ossification · Growth plates · Fracture classification & healing · Synovial joints · Osteoarthritis

Core sequence: mesenchyme → osteoblast/bone directly (intramembranous) OR mesenchyme → cartilage → calcification → vascular invasion → bone (endochondral). Fracture soft callus follows the second route.

Two ossification pathways

FeatureIntramembranousEndochondral
Starting materialMesenchyme → osteoblasts → osteoid → mineralized woven boneMesenchyme → hyaline cartilage template → hypertrophy/calcification → vessel invasion → bone
Major locationsFlat bones / skull; periosteal depositionLong, short, irregular bones; physis
LandmarksOssification centers → trabeculae → compact cortex; woven → lamellarBone collar → primary diaphyseal center → marrow cavity → secondary epiphyseal centers
ClinicalDistraction osteogenesis; rigidly stabilized direct bone healingLongitudinal growth; fracture soft callus; MO/FOP heterotopic bone

Growth plate · epiphysis → metaphysis

Zone / stepHistology / eventAssociation
RestingReserve chondrocytes; scattered small cellsPhysis = epiphyseal/growth plate
ProliferationMitosis; ordered flattened stacksLongitudinal growth
HypertrophyCells enlarge; lacunae expandPrepares matrix for calcification
Death / calcificationCalcified cartilage; capillaries invadeOsteoprogenitors enter
Deposition / resorptionWoven bone on cartilage cores → remodelingBone length increases until physeal closure
Length = physis; diameter = periosteal/endosteal apposition. Physis closure ends further lengthening.

DisorderDefect / mechanismRapid clue
AchondroplasiaConstitutively active FGFR3 → narrow/disordered proliferative + hypertrophic zonesShort limbs, relatively normal trunk, enlarged head

Fracture repair · order and timing

StageCore eventTiming (approximate)
HematomaRuptured vessels; inflammationHours → days
GranulationCapillaries + fibroblasts / mesenchyme~2–12 days
Soft callusFibrocartilage stabilizes ends~1 week → months
Hard callusCartilage replaced with woven bone via endochondral routeWeeks → months
RemodelingWoven → lamellar; callus reducedMonths → years
Stages overlap. Highly rigid fixation may permit direct intramembranous healing without a soft cartilage callus.

Fracture classifications · identify in seconds

AxisTypes and defining distinction
LineTransverse = across; oblique = diagonal; longitudinal = along shaft; spiral = torsion
SpecialGreenstick: one cortex bends, other breaks · Torus: buckled cortex · Compression: crushed/shortened vertebra
PositionDisplacement = offset · Angulation = abnormal axis · Shortening = overlap · Rotation = twist
FragmentsSimple = 2 · Comminuted = >2
SkinClosed = no skin communication · Open/compound = communicates with atmosphere, even without visible bone

Synovial joint · structure → function

PartRole / clue
Hyaline articular cartilageSmooth, low friction; avascular, no perichondrium → limited repair
Fibrous capsuleOuter dense CT; localized thickening forms ligaments
Synovial membraneLines inner capsule, not articular cartilage

OA · mechanism → radiograph

What failsRecognize / consequence
Cartilage matrixProteoglycans, water, type II collagen fibrils disrupted → tears / thinning
Whole-joint changesCartilage + synovium + capsule + subchondral bone affected
RadiographNarrow joint space + subchondral sclerosis + osteophytes; deformation in advanced disease
Risk factorsAge, joint injury/overuse, female, obesity, genetics
Management taughtSevere disease → total joint arthroplasty
FIRST AID CONNECTION OA often presents with mechanical pain and brief post-rest stiffness. EXAM TRAP OA is not initially a primarily inflammatory arthritis.

Don’t mix these up

MO vs FOP
Local post-traumatic, often self-limited MO ≠ progressive genetic ACVR1 FOP.
Greenstick vs buckle
Greenstick = one side breaks; buckle = cortex compressed/buckles.
Vertebroplasty vs kyphoplasty
Direct cement vs balloon cavity, then cement.
Joint membrane vs cartilage
Synovium lines capsule, not the avascular hyaline cartilage.

Rapid-fire recall

1. No cartilage template? Intramembranous
2. Growth plate proliferative zone? Mitosis / columns
3. Initial fracture bridge? Hematoma → granulation → soft callus
4. Torque injury? Spiral fracture
5. >2 bone fragments? Comminuted
6. Genetic heterotopic bone gene? ACVR1
7. Cartilage lubrication molecules? Hyaluronic acid + lubricin
8. OA x-ray triad? Narrowing + sclerosis + osteophytes
