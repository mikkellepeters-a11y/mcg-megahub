Learning objective 1

Localize the source of musculoskeletal pain

First determine where the pathology is occurring. Articular disease originates in subchondral bone, cartilage, synovial fluid, synovium or joint capsule. Periarticular disease involves structures around the joint (tendon, tenosynovium, enthesis, bursa, ligament). Nonarticular pain can arise in muscle or bone; referred pain may come from visceral, vascular or neurologic sources.

Apply to the opening case: Joint-line tenderness throughout MCPs/wrists, pain with active and passive movement, and a wrist effusion strongly favor an articular inflammatory process, not focal tendon disease.

Learning objective 3

Approach arthritis by timing, pattern and distribution

Timing

Acute <6 weeks vs chronic >6 weeks; episodic vs persistent; migratory vs additive; rapid vs insidious.

Pattern

Monoarticular 1; oligoarticular ≤4; polyarticular >4. Also assess symmetry, small vs large joints, and axial involvement.

Distribution

Which exact joints? A symmetric small-joint pattern (MCP, PIP, wrists) suggests RA; DIP/1st CMC and weight-bearing joints favor OA. Assess spinal distribution: the lecture teaches RA spinal involvement at C1–C2 only, whereas OA may affect the cervical and lumbar spine.

Clinical reasoning example

The lecture patient has four months of persistent, symmetric, polyarticular MCP/wrist inflammation, morning stiffness lasting two hours, fatigue, erosions and highly positive RF/anti-CCP. This supports rheumatoid arthritis.

Characteristic RA hand deformities

Ulnar drift

MCP inflammation and ligament/tendon injury → ulnar deviation of fingers, often with radial wrist deviation.

Swan neck

PIP hyperextension and DIP flexion.

Boutonnière

PIP flexion and DIP hyperextension.

Hitchhiker / Z-thumb

First MCP flexion and IP hyperextension.

High-stakes C1–C2 complication: Transverse-ligament weakening → atlantoaxial subluxation → spinal cord or vertebral artery compromise. Lecture's cutoff: >3 mm abnormal. Neck/occipital pain, electric shocks with flexion, paresthesias, drop attacks, dizziness or syncope require attention; lecture calls for flexion/extension cervical radiographs in symptomatic RA or prior to surgery/intubation.
Learning objective 5

Explain rheumatoid arthritis pathogenesis

RA arises from interplay of genetics, epigenetics and mucosal/environmental triggers. The lecture highlights smoking, periodontal disease and the gut microbiome. Peptidylarginine deiminase (PAD) converts arginine → citrulline in self-proteins, potentially breaking self-tolerance and generating anti-citrullinated protein antibodies.

Genetic association: HLA-DRB1 shared epitope alleles, including HLA-DRB1*04 and *01, are risk factors. Smoking and genetic susceptibility interact synergistically in the lecture's model.

Inflammatory cells infiltrate the synovium → cytokines and chemokines recruit further cells → synovial lining expands to create pannus → collagenases/metalloproteinases damage cartilage → osteoclast activation causes marginal bone erosions.

Learning objective 6

Recognize extra-articular RA and basic treatment

Rheumatoid nodules

About 25%; extensor forearms, olecranon bursa and pressure points. Pathology: central fibrinoid necrosis surrounded by palisading histiocytes.

Pulmonary

Subcutaneous-like pulmonary nodules; Caplan syndrome = nodules + pneumoconiosis. Interstitial lung disease (fibrosis; ground glass, reticulation, honeycombing/traction bronchiectasis), pleuritis/effusions (often exudative, very low glucose).

Cardiac and vascular

Systemic inflammation increases coronary artery disease risk; vasculitis and other systemic effects also discussed.

Eye

Episcleritis: mild discomfort, globe nontender; topical phenylephrine rapidly blanches redness. Scleritis: severe deep pain/tender eye, incomplete blanching. Peripheral ulcerative keratitis: corneal thinning, photophobia/decreased vision.

Felty syndrome

Classic triad: RA + neutropenia + splenomegaly. Can include lower-extremity ulcers, fever and recurrent infection; rare, usually long-standing RA.

Risk factors for extra-articular disease

Lecture lists RF/anti-CCP positivity, HLA-DR4 and male sex.

Methotrexate at RA doses

Lecture describes first-line treatment when no contraindications: 15–25 mg every 7 days orally or subcutaneously; anti-inflammatory activity involves inhibition of AICAR transformylase and increased adenosine. Give daily folic acid to mitigate some adverse effects. Common toxicities: nausea/GI upset, stomatitis/oral ulcers, leukopenia, alopecia, hepatotoxicity. Rare/important: teratogenicity, pneumonitis and accelerated rheumatoid nodulosis.

Dosing safety: Weekly — not daily — methotrexate dosing is essential. Specific prescribing and monitoring require clinician oversight.
Learning objective 7

Rapid-fire retrieval checkpoints

What finding distinguishes intra-articular from periarticular pain?

Articular pain affects the entire joint line and hurts with active AND passive ROM; periarticular pain is more focal and generally worsens more with active than passive motion.

Morning stiffness: RA vs OA?

RA typically >1 hour and improves with use; OA ≤30 minutes and worsens with activity.

Minimum synovial aspirate panel?

Cell count with differential, crystal analysis, Gram stain, aerobic and anaerobic bacterial culture.

Inflammatory versus septic synovial WBC ranges from the lecture?

Inflammatory 2,000–50,000/mm³ and >50% PMNs; septic often >50,000/mm³ and >75% PMNs.

Which crystals are negatively versus positively birefringent?

MSU = needles, strongly negative; CPPD = rhomboids/rods, weakly positive.

Which hand joints favor RA vs OA?

RA favors MCP, PIP and wrists; OA favors DIP, PIP and first CMC.

RA vs OA radiograph discriminator?

RA = periarticular osteopenia and marginal erosions; OA = osteophytes, subchondral sclerosis/cysts and asymmetric narrowing.

How does the HLA-DRB1 shared epitope connect to smoking?

The lecture emphasizes a synergistic risk for RA, potentially mediated by responses to citrullinated self-proteins.

What is pannus?

Hyperplastic inflammatory synovial tissue that damages cartilage and activates bone erosion.

Swan neck versus boutonnière?

Swan neck: PIP hyperextension / DIP flexion. Boutonnière: PIP flexion / DIP hyperextension.

What is Felty syndrome?

RA + neutropenia + splenomegaly.

What is the methotrexate schedule emphasized?

15–25 mg once every 7 days, with daily folic acid per lecture.
