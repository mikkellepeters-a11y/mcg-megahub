Smooth Muscle Physiology — Factoid

MSK-Skin · Week 17 · Lecture 4 · Ruchi Patel, PhD · Course-first rapid review
Anchor set: Non-striated + dense bodies + no troponin · Single-unit = gap junctions · ↑ Ca²⁺ → calmodulin → MLCK → phosphorylated myosin → contraction · MLCP + ↓ Ca²⁺ → relaxation · α₁/Gq constricts · NO/cGMP dilates.

Structure & function

  • Spindle-shaped, single central nucleus, no sarcomeres / T-tubules.
  • Actin + myosin + tropomyosin; no troponin; filaments anchor to dense bodies.
  • Hollow-organ walls → squeeze, peristalsis, regulate luminal diameter, resist internal load.
  • Sites: gut, urinary organs, vessels, airway, eye, piloerectors.

Single- vs multi-unit

  • Single-unit: gap junctions; cells contract together, often spontaneous; gut, urinary, vascular (lecture examples).
  • Multi-unit: discrete densely innervated fibers; vas deferens, iris, piloerectors.
  • Clue: coupled sheets versus individually controlled bundles.

Ca²⁺ contraction chain

  • ↑ cytosolic Ca²⁺ (ECF entry + SR release).
  • Ca²⁺–calmodulin activates MLCK.
  • MLCK uses ATP to phosphorylate myosin light chain.
  • Myosin binds actin → cross-bridge power stroke; ATP required for detachment.
  • MLCP removes phosphate; lower Ca²⁺ permits relaxation.

Activation / action potentials

  • ANS/enteric transmitter, hormones, local chemical factors, stretch or spontaneous electrical activity.
  • Depolarization opens L-type Ca²⁺ channels; receptors may also regulate Ca²⁺ entry/release.
  • Unitary APs: stimulus spike, slow-wave-triggered repetitive spikes, plateau AP (prolonged contraction).
  • Lecture reports resting potential ~55 mV; physiologic resting polarity is negative.

Diffuse autonomic junctions

  • Axonal varicosities release transmitter into diffuse junctions; not a skeletal motor end plate.
  • Receptor determines whether transmitter excites or inhibits.
  • Epinephrine → α₁: many vessels contract; epinephrine → β₂: bronchioles relax.

Four sources of tone regulation

  • Neural: NE, ACh; sympathetic, parasympathetic, enteric.
  • Hormonal: epinephrine, CCK, angiotensin II.
  • Local: histamine, H⁺, O₂, CO₂, NO, ions.
  • Mechanical: stretch → gated ion channels → depolarization.
  • Basal tone = some MLC phosphorylation at rest.

Vasoconstriction

  • ↑ Ca²⁺ entry/release → CaM/MLCK → myosin phosphorylation.
  • ↓ Ca²⁺ clearance, ↓ NO or ↓ cAMP/cGMP favors constriction.
  • α₁, AT₁, V₁: Gq; α₂, 5-HT₁: Gi (lecture receptor list).

Vasodilation

  • NO → ↑ cGMP; Gs → ↑ cAMP.
  • Hyperpolarization → ↓ L-type Ca²⁺ entry.
  • ↑ SR sequestration / Ca²⁺ extrusion.
  • A₂A and H₂ are lecture-listed Gs dilator receptors.
  • PDE inhibition prevents cyclic nucleotide breakdown.

Exercise paradox

  • Exercise → sympathetic vasoconstrictor drive ↑.
  • Working muscle → ↓ O₂, ↑ CO₂, ↑ H⁺ and local metabolites.
  • Local vasodilation counteracts sympathetic constriction → blood flow rises where needed.

Energy, latch & length–tension

  • Latch mechanism: very slow cross-bridge cycling/delayed detachment sustains tone with low ATP cost.
  • Smooth muscle uses much less energy for sustained tension than skeletal muscle.
  • Operates across a wider range of lengths than skeletal muscle.

FIRST AID RAPID CONNECTIONS

  • NO → cGMP → vascular relaxation.
  • Nitrates: increase NO signaling.
  • PDE inhibitors: maintain cyclic nucleotide signaling.
  • Ca²⁺ channel blockers: limit Ca²⁺ influx; reduce vascular smooth-muscle tone.
  • α₁ vs β₂: receptor subtype predicts tissue response.

RAPID-FIRE RECALL

  1. Absent regulator? Troponin.
  2. Thin filament anchor? Dense bodies.
  3. Coupled muscle type? Single-unit.
  4. Ca²⁺ receptor? Calmodulin.
  5. Myosin phosphorylation enzyme? MLCK.
  6. Myosin dephosphorylation enzyme? MLCP.
  7. Contractile Gq vascular receptor? α₁.
  8. NO second messenger? cGMP.
  9. Exercise local dilators? ↓O₂, ↑CO₂, ↑H⁺.
  10. Energy-saving sustained tone? Latch.
