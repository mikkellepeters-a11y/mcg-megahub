Essential physiology recall

Excitation–contraction in six links

  • α-motor neuron → ACh release at NMJ; nicotinic receptor → end-plate potential.
  • VG Na⁺ channels propagate muscle AP down sarcolemma/T-tubule.
  • DHP voltage sensor mechanically activates RyR → Ca²⁺ exits SR.
  • Ca²⁺ binds troponin → tropomyosin uncovers actin sites.
  • Myosin ATPase cycles cross bridges; ADP/Pi released in power stroke.
  • SERCA re-sequesters Ca²⁺ → relaxation.

Twitch timing

  • Latent phase: AP, T-tubule signaling, SR Ca²⁺ release.
  • Contraction: cross bridges develop force.
  • Relaxation: SERCA lowers cytosolic Ca²⁺.
  • Electrical AP is much briefer than mechanical twitch.

Mechanics and force production

Four levers of force

Active vs passive tension

  • Active: cross bridges; too short/too long reduces overlap effectiveness.
  • Passive: titin/elastic element stretch; rises at longer lengths.
  • Total: includes both components.
  • Power: force × velocity; peak at intermediate load/speed (~⅓ max velocity shown in lecture).

Recruitment vs stimulation

  • Size principle: small low-threshold units first, large high-threshold units later.
  • Motor units typically have uniform fiber type.
  • Frequency summation is repeated activation of an already active motor unit.
  • High frequency → fused tetanus (mechanical); APs themselves do not fuse.

Remodeling distinctions

  • Hypertrophy: larger existing myofibers, common with resistance training.
  • Hyperplasia: new fibers; rare.
  • Lengthening: greater shortening range and max velocity; not increased force.
  • Atrophy: reduced mass/strength from disuse, denervation, aging, malnutrition, cachexia.

Selective board bridges

DHP/RyR dysregulation → malignant hyperthermia → dantrolene limits calcium release. Glycogen utilization failure → McArdle disease → exertional cramps/intolerance. These are explicitly supplemental; neither diagnosis is presented as a core item on Patel’s lecture slides.

RAPID-FIRE RECALL

  1. Which structure detects T-tubule depolarization?

    DHP receptor, mechanically activating RyR in skeletal muscle.

  2. Which ion binds troponin?

    Ca²⁺.

  3. What causes cross-bridge detachment?

    New ATP binding to myosin.

  4. Which sarcomere band never shortens?

    A band.

  5. What is the size principle?

    Recruit small low-threshold motor units before large high-threshold ones.

  6. What produces fused tetanus?

    Very frequent APs with summating mechanical twitches.

  7. Why does a stretched fiber develop passive tension?

    Stretch of titin and other elastic elements.

  8. What does increased afterload do to velocity?

    Decreases shortening velocity.

  9. What two metabolic changes support endurance?

    More oxidative mitochondria/enzymes and increased capillary density.

  10. How does hypertrophy differ from hyperplasia?

    Larger existing fibers versus more fibers.
