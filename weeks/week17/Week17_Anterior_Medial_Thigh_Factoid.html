Anterior and Medial Thigh — Factoid Sheet

MSK-Skin · Week 17 · Lecture 1 · Manuel · Course-first rapid review
Last-minute framework: anterior thigh = hip flexion / knee extension (femoral); medial thigh = adduction (obturator); adductor magnus hamstring = sciatic. Femoral triangle NAVEL; nerve OUTSIDE sheath. Adductor hiatus: femoral a. → popliteal a.

Bone map

  • Os coxae: ilium + ischium + pubis → acetabulum.
  • ASIS: sartorius + inguinal ligament. AIIS: rectus femoris.
  • Femur: head/neck, trochanters, linea aspera, condyles, epicondyles, adductor tubercle.
  • Ischial tuberosity: hamstring part of adductor magnus.

Anterior thigh — action

  • Anterior: hip flexion / knee extension. Predominantly femoral n.
  • Sartorius: flex, abduct, laterally rotate hip; flex knee → pes anserinus.
  • Quadriceps: rectus femoris + three vasti → knee extension.
  • Rectus femoris: also hip flexion; starts at AIIS.

Quadriceps extensor mechanism

  • Quadriceps tendon → patella → patellar ligament → tibial tuberosity.
  • Vastus lateralis: greater trochanter/linea aspera.
  • Vastus medialis: intertrochanteric line/linea aspera.
  • Vastus intermedius: anterior/lateral femoral shaft.

Anterior exceptions

  • Iliopsoas: psoas major (T12–L5; anterior rami) + iliacus (iliac fossa; femoral n.) → lesser trochanter; hip flexion.
  • Pectineus: superior pubic ramus → pectineal line; flex/adduct hip and assist lateral rotation; femoral n. (lecture).

Medial adductors

  • Obturator n.: gracilis, adductor longus, adductor brevis, adductor portion of magnus, obturator externus.
  • Gracilis: pubis → pes anserinus; hip adduction; knee flexion/medial rotation.
  • Longus: pubis → linea aspera. Brevis: pubis → pectineal line/linea aspera.
  • Obturator externus: lateral hip rotation.

Adductor magnus — split

  • Adductor part: inferior pubic ramus → gluteal tuberosity/linea aspera; obturator n.; adduction.
  • Hamstring part: ischial tuberosity → adductor tubercle; sciatic n.; hip extension.
  • Adductor hiatus: femoral vessels enter popliteal fossa; femoral a. → popliteal a.

Pes anserinus

  • Common medial tibial attachment: Sartorius (anterior), Gracilis (medial), Semitendinosus (posterior).
  • One muscle from each thigh compartment.

Fascia & iliotibial tract

  • Fascia lata + lateral, medial and posterior septa divide compartments.
  • IT tract: thick lateral fascia; receives gluteus maximus + tensor fasciae latae fibers.

Femoral triangle — NAVEL

  • Superior: inguinal ligament; lateral: sartorius; medial: adductor longus.
  • Floor: iliopsoas + pectineus; roof: fascia lata.
  • Lateral → medial: Nerve, Artery, Vein, Empty (canal), Lymphatics.

Femoral sheath / hernia

  • Sheath: artery (lateral), vein (intermediate), femoral canal (medial; fat + lymphatics).
  • Femoral nerve NOT in sheath. Femoral ring is proximal canal opening.
  • Femoral hernia: viscera enter canal; rigid ring → strangulation → bowel ischemia/necrosis.

Adductor canal

  • Inferior femoral triangle → adductor hiatus.
  • Lateral: vastus medialis; medial/roof: sartorius; posterior: adductor longus/magnus.
  • Contents: femoral artery + vein + saphenous nerve.

Lumbar plexus & nerves

  • Femoral L2–L4: anterior thigh motor; anterior cutaneous and saphenous sensory.
  • Obturator L2–L4: medial thigh motor; passes obturator canal.
  • Lateral femoral cutaneous L2–L3: sensory ONLY to anterolateral thigh.
  • Dermatomes: anterior thigh primarily L2/L3 in lecture.

Femoral arterial path

  • External iliac → femoral beneath inguinal ligament → popliteal through adductor hiatus.
  • Profunda femoris: medial + lateral circumflex femoral branches.
  • Medial circumflex compromise → femoral-head avascular necrosis (lecture).

Great saphenous & varicosities

  • Medial dorsal venous arch → anterior to medial malleolus → medial leg/thigh → femoral v. via saphenous opening.
  • Varicose veins: dilation + valve failure → reflux; clots may develop.

FIRST AID RAPID CONNECTIONS

  • Femoral n. / quadriceps → patellar reflex L3–L4.
  • Medial circumflex disruption after femoral neck injury → osteonecrosis.
  • Lateral femoral cutaneous entrapment → meralgia paresthetica, no weakness.
  • Femoral hernia vs inguinal: below vs above inguinal ligament.
  • Pes anserine bursitis → focal anteromedial proximal tibial pain.

RAPID-FIRE RECALL

ASIS attachment?
Sartorius (plus inguinal ligament).
AIIS attachment?
Rectus femoris.
4 quadriceps heads?
Rectus femoris; vastus medialis, lateralis, intermedius.
NAVEL order?
Femoral nerve, artery, vein, empty femoral canal, lymphatics.
Which NAVEL member is outside the femoral sheath?
Femoral nerve.
Which adductor magnus component uses sciatic nerve?
Hamstring part.
Pes anserinus three?
Sartorius, gracilis, semitendinosus.
What enters the adductor hiatus?
Femoral artery and vein; artery becomes popliteal.
Pure sensory anterolateral thigh nerve?
Lateral femoral cutaneous L2–L3.
Great saphenous relation to malleolus?
Anterior to medial malleolus.
