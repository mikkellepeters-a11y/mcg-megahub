Metabolic Myopathies — Factoid

MSK-Skin · Week 17 · Wednesday Lecture 1 · Barnes · Professor-first rapid review
One-line algorithm: sprint cramps ± second wind → glycogen; endurance + fasting + delayed pigmenturia → fatty acid oxidation; endurance + ptosis/hearing/GI findings → mitochondrial.

Core myopathic pattern

  • Proximal > distal weakness; normal sensation and usually normal reflexes.
  • Exercise intolerance, myalgia/cramps, fatigue; may have waddling gait/hyperlordosis.
  • Metabolic clue = episodic symptoms with exercise, fasting or illness; exam can normalize between episodes.

Trigger timing = first discriminator

  • Seconds–minutes, sprint/intense: glycogen storage/glycolytic defect.
  • Long-duration exercise, fasting, illness: fatty acid oxidation or mitochondrial.
  • Multisystem eye/ear/GI/brain signs favor mitochondria.

McArdle (GSD V)

  • PYGM = muscle glycogen phosphorylase deficiency.
  • Early exertional cramps; second wind after rest/lower intensity.
  • Persistent CK elevation; myogenic hyperuricemia/gout; possible exercise rhabdomyolysis.

Glycolytic defects: Tarui

  • Tarui / GSD VII: phosphofructokinase deficiency.
  • Intense exercise cramps, typically no second wind.
  • Review notes fasting can improve exercise tolerance in some glycolytic defects; unlike McArdle.

Carbohydrate defect testing

  • Forearm nonischemic test: flat/low lactate rise + increased ammonia.
  • EMG/NCS often normal; genetic panels and muscle enzyme analysis as indicated.
  • McArdle can show persistent CK; uric acid may be high.

Fatty acid oxidation defects

  • CPT II, VLCAD, carnitine transport pathway and related defects.
  • Prolonged exercise or fasting → delayed myalgia, pigmenturia and rhabdomyolysis.
  • Usually autosomal recessive; CK often normal between events.

Fat oxidation tests

  • Serum acylcarnitine profile especially useful in fasting/after exercise.
  • Urine dicarboxylic acids ↑ in beta-oxidation defects; hypoketotic hypoglycemia during an event.
  • Biopsy may show neutral lipid but may be normal.

Mitochondrial phenotype

  • Impaired ETC/mitochondrial metabolism; prolonged exercise/illness/fasting triggers.
  • Ptosis, ophthalmoparesis, hearing/vision deficits, GI complaints, sometimes fixed weakness.
  • mtDNA maternal inheritance possible; nuclear mitochondrial genes also cause disease.

Mitochondrial testing

  • Lactate elevated in ~65% (lecture review); normal result does not exclude.
  • Low VO₂max/high respiratory exchange ratio; muscle ragged red or COX-negative fibers.
  • Blood mtDNA panel can be falsely negative; muscle-derived DNA may be diagnostic.

Acute rhabdomyolysis

  • CK >10× ULN with muscle injury; dark urine can reflect myoglobin.
  • Check renal function and electrolytes; risk of kidney injury.
  • Repeat attacks + childhood exercise intolerance justify detailed metabolic evaluation.

Management: glycogen diseases

  • Carefully progressive exercise; pre-exercise sucrose/glucose for McArdle.
  • Hyperuricemia/gout management; selected supplements listed in review with varying evidence.
  • For some glycolytic defects, fasting can improve tolerance.

Management: FAO / mitochondria

  • FAO: avoid fasting, illness exercise; carbohydrate-rich dietary support; carnitine only if low.
  • Mitochondria: gradual activity, avoid fasting/illness; review describes coenzyme Q10/other cocktail options.
  • Triheptanoin discussed for selected severe FAO patients subject to availability.

FIRST AID CONNECTION

  • First Aid 2025 biochemistry pp. 84–85: McArdle → muscle myophosphorylase, muscle glycogen accumulation.
  • Exercise painful cramps + myoglobinuria with normal systemic glucose.
  • Flat venous lactate curve, ammonia rise with exercise.

FIRST AID RAPID CONNECTIONS

  • McArdle: muscle phosphorylase → cramps, second wind, exercise myoglobinuria (First Aid).
  • Forearm exercise: flat lactate with increased ammonia → carbohydrate-pathway defect (First Aid).
  • Hepatic vs muscle glycogen: McArdle often preserves blood glucose (First Aid).

RAPID-FIRE RECALL

  1. Early intense exercise cramps + second wind? McArdle (PYGM).
  2. Which metabolite fails to increase on forearm exercise? Lactate.
  3. Which metabolite may rise despite flat lactate? Ammonia.
  4. Endurance/fasting-triggered rhabdomyolysis, first-line metabolic profile? Acylcarnitines.
  5. Hypoketotic hypoglycemia suggests? Fatty acid oxidation defect.
  6. Exercise intolerance + ptosis/hearing loss? Mitochondrial myopathy.
  7. Ragged red + COX-negative? Mitochondrial biopsy pathology.
  8. Negative blood mtDNA excludes disease? No—consider muscle DNA.
  9. Preexercise sucrose is especially useful for? McArdle.
