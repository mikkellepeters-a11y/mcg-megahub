OT for Hand Problems — Factoid Sheet

MSK–Skin · Week 17 · Hall · Week 14 canonical factoid layout
ANCHOR SET: Meaningful occupation → 5 domains (pain · ROM · strength · edema · sensation/dexterity) → targeted therapy + adapted tasks → MD-approved plan, frequent reassessment.

Five domains → treatment choices

MUST KNOW · OT purpose

  • Restore meaningful occupations (ADLs, work, play, hobbies), not just a strength score.
  • Goals: support healing/surgical outcomes; restore patient factors; improve quality of movement.
  • Pain-free/limited-pain function (lecture example <3/10).

1 · Pain

  • Heat, fluidotherapy, cold compress.
  • Nerve glides for nerve pain; support-muscle strengthening for tendinitis.
  • Massage for spasms; desensitization; adaptive equipment.

2 · ROM

  • AROM active; AAROM assisted; PROM passive.
  • Splinting, flexion glove; TheraBand loop for PIP contracture.
  • Scar adhesion management: ultrasound, silicone, scar massage.

3 · Strength

  • Isometric, concentric and eccentric exercise.
  • Thera-putty, TheraBands, Digi-Flex, Thera-web, Thera-bar.
  • Train relevant grip/pinch and task endurance.

4 · Edema

  • Gentle compression: Coban, Tubigrip, edema glove.
  • Elevated exercise = edema pumping; contrast baths.
  • Counterpressure can relieve edema-associated ache.

5 · Sensation / coordination / dexterity

  • Nerve injury can disrupt sensory feedback and safe hand use.
  • Splint positioning, nerve glides and modalities are lecture-listed approaches.
  • Compensate using vision, repeated practice and adapted tools.

Splinting, adaptive equipment, and team care

Splint design

  • Commonly custom thermoplastic with hook-and-loop closure.
  • Protect selected structures OR stress/stretch intended tissues.
  • Comfort → adherence; prefabricated/static-progressive choices for contracture when appropriate.

Adaptive equipment (AE)

  • Arthritis → weak/painful grip → jar-opening tools.
  • Remediation + compensation together preserve independence.
  • Complex requirements → robotic hand, coordinated with orthotics representatives.

OT–physician workflow

  • Functional problem → PCP/orthopedist → referral → OT evaluation → plan → MD signature → therapy.
  • OT needs a prescription; postoperative protocol agreed in advance.
  • Reevaluate and report significant change or unexpected recovery course.

De Quervain care coordination

  • Slide example: wrist pain 6/10 → 4/10 and localizes to first dorsal compartment.
  • Positive Finkelstein → OT communicates concern to referring physician.
  • Do not silently change diagnosis or surgeon-directed protocol.

Case-based recall

Finger cases

  • Volar plate repair + immobilization + minimal rehab → PIP flexion contracture.
  • RA with PIP flexion + DIP hyperextension → boutonnière, central slip dysfunction.
  • Elson: rigid/extended DIP with PIP fixed at 90° is positive per lecture.

Hand cases

  • Carpal tunnel release → persistent radial/palmar pillar pain: assess five domains.
  • OA/RA → rest, inflammation control, heat/cold, gentle ROM/strength. Do not push through pain.
  • LRTI referral considered in lecture for selected thumb-base arthritis.

Wrist cases

  • Housekeeper: repetitive 6–8-hour workdays → detailed occupation-specific interview.
  • Distal radius ORIF → protocol-led ROM/strength progression for tools and computer work.
  • Wrist OA: lecture considers ~6-week conservative period, potential proximal-row carpectomy if unsuccessful.

Elbow cases

  • Tennis pain → activity modification, graded recovery.
  • Child post-supraconydlar fracture → motion, strength and return-to-gymnastics readiness after clearance.
  • RA flare → gentle symptom management + rheumatology communication.
