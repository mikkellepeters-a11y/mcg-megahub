BIG PICTURE. Smooth muscle uses actin–myosin sliding just as skeletal muscle does, but controls access to force production differently: cytosolic Ca²⁺ activates calmodulin → myosin light-chain kinase (MLCK) → myosin phosphorylation. Its ability to respond to nerves, hormones, local metabolites and stretch makes it ideal for hollow organs and vascular tone.

Official learning objectives

  1. Describe general structure, function and location of smooth muscle types.
  2. Describe excitation–contraction coupling and the role of Ca²⁺, calmodulin, myosin kinase and myosin phosphatase; contrast with skeletal muscle.
  3. Describe nervous, hormonal and chemical regulation of smooth muscle.
  4. Explain physiologic dilator and constrictor pathways in vascular smooth muscle.

1 · Structure, location and function

MUST KNOW · LEARNING OBJECTIVE 1

Smooth-muscle cells are spindle-shaped, non-striated, with a single central nucleus. They contain actin, myosin and tropomyosin, but no troponin. Thin filaments attach to cytoplasmic dense bodies and membrane attachment sites. Filaments are not assembled into sarcomeres or myofibrils; contraction therefore shortens and distorts the cell without visible striations.

In walls of hollow organs, this structural arrangement produces a squeezing action that changes organ volume, opposes internal pressure, moves contents by peristalsis or changes the caliber of a tube. Course examples include the gut, urinary/reproductive systems, blood and lymph vessels, airway, ciliary muscle/iris and arrector pili.

RETRIEVAL CHECKPOINT · Why can single-unit cells contract together?

Gap junctions electrically couple neighboring cells, allowing their activity to spread through the sheet.

2 · Excitation–contraction coupling: Ca²⁺ turns on myosin

MUST KNOW · LEARNING OBJECTIVE 2

Unlike skeletal muscle, smooth muscle can be activated by autonomic/enteric neurotransmitters, spontaneous electrical activity, hormones, local factors or stretch-induced opening of mechanically gated channels. Electrical depolarization opens L-type voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channels, whereas chemical signals may open channels through receptor-mediated pathways. Both extracellular Ca²⁺ entry and sarcoplasmic reticulum Ca²⁺ release contribute to the rise in cytosolic Ca²⁺.

Stimulus → ↑ cytosolic Ca²⁺ → Ca²⁺–calmodulin → MLCK activation → myosin light-chain phosphorylation (ATP used) → actin–myosin cycling → contraction

Step by step: (1) Ca²⁺ rises; (2) Ca²⁺ binds calmodulin; (3) Ca²⁺–calmodulin activates MLCK; (4) MLCK phosphorylates the myosin regulatory light chain with ATP; (5) phosphorylated myosin engages actin and cycles. ATP is also needed for myosin detachment. Relaxation requires falling intracellular Ca²⁺ and myosin light-chain phosphatase (MLCP) removing myosin phosphate. The core regulatory target is myosin, not troponin on actin.

Electrical behavior: Single-unit smooth muscle may generate an externally elicited spike, slow-wave-triggered repetitive spikes or an action potential with a plateau (prolonged contraction). The lecture emphasizes voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channels rather than fast voltage-gated Na⁺ channels in these action potentials. The slide labels the resting potential “~55 mV”; physiologically the intended resting polarity is negative (approximately −55 mV).

Latch mechanism: Cross-bridge cycling can remain slow while tension persists, minimizing ATP consumption. The lecture contrasts smooth muscle's low energy consumption and broad operating range of lengths with skeletal muscle's faster, more energy-demanding contraction and narrower length–tension range.

RETRIEVAL CHECKPOINT · What directly activates smooth-muscle myosin, and what relaxes it?

Ca²⁺–calmodulin activates MLCK; MLCK phosphorylates myosin. Falling Ca²⁺ plus MLCP-mediated dephosphorylation supports relaxation.

RETRIEVAL CHECKPOINT · Why does an L-type Ca²⁺ blocker generally reduce smooth-muscle contraction?

Less extracellular Ca²⁺ enters, reducing Ca²⁺–calmodulin activation of MLCK and downstream myosin phosphorylation.

3 · Neural, hormonal and local regulation

MUST KNOW · LEARNING OBJECTIVE 3

Smooth muscle frequently maintains basal tone through low-level myosin light-chain phosphorylation. The final contraction level reflects competing signals, not an all-or-none command. Autonomic axons release neurotransmitter from varicosities into diffuse junctions, unlike the specialized skeletal neuromuscular junction. A transmitter may excite one tissue and inhibit another because receptor subtype and intracellular signaling determine the effect.

COMMON CONFUSIONEpinephrine is not universally contractile. The lecture contrasts α₁-mediated contraction in many vascular beds with β₂-mediated relaxation of bronchiolar smooth muscle. Always identify the receptor and tissue before predicting direction.
RETRIEVAL CHECKPOINT · Why can epinephrine constrict one smooth muscle and relax another?

Different receptor subtypes trigger different intracellular signals: α₁ generally promotes contraction, whereas β₂ in airways promotes relaxation.

4 · Vascular tone: the balance of constrictors and dilators

MUST KNOW · LEARNING OBJECTIVE 4

Vasoconstriction

  • ↑ Ca²⁺ entry (membrane depolarization) or SR release
  • Activate Gq pathways and Ca²⁺–calmodulin/MLCK
  • Inhibit NO signaling
  • Reduce Ca²⁺ uptake/extrusion
  • Decrease cAMP/cGMP signaling

Vasodilation

  • ↑ NO
  • Hyperpolarize membrane → inhibit Ca²⁺ entry
  • ↑ Ca²⁺ uptake into SR / extrusion from cell
  • ↑ cGMP or cAMP, including by inhibiting phosphodiesterase
  • Favor MLCP activity / reduce contractile signaling

CLINICAL / PHYSIOLOGIC CONNECTION · EXERCISE

Exercise increases sympathetic vasoconstrictor output, yet active skeletal muscle needs greater perfusion. In working muscle, ↓ O₂, ↑ CO₂ and ↑ H⁺ plus other local metabolic factors generate vasodilator drive that counteracts sympathetic vasoconstriction. The net effect is increased flow where metabolic demand rises.

FIRST AID CONNECTION · SELECTIVE STEP 1 BRIDGE

Nitrates/NO–cGMP: NO stimulates guanylyl cyclase and increases cGMP, relaxing vascular smooth muscle. Phosphodiesterase inhibitors: preserve cyclic nucleotide signaling. Calcium-channel blockers: reduce L-type Ca²⁺ entry into vascular smooth muscle. These are pharmacologic applications of the lecture's own signaling pathways, not additional assigned lecture drugs.

RETRIEVAL CHECKPOINT · If cGMP rises in vascular smooth muscle, what happens to vascular caliber?

cGMP favors relaxation of vascular smooth muscle, so vessel caliber generally increases (vasodilation).

RETRIEVAL CHECKPOINT · Why can local muscle blood flow rise during sympathetic activation?

Local metabolic vasodilators from working muscle oppose sympathetic constriction and permit increased perfusion.

EXAM TRAPThe regulatory fork is skeletal/cardiac: Ca²⁺ → troponin → actin accessibility, versus smooth: Ca²⁺ → calmodulin → MLCK → phosphorylated myosin. Smooth muscle also has no sarcomeres or transverse tubules.

MASTER THESE BEFORE MOVING ON

  1. Structural design: dense bodies replace sarcomere-style anchoring, enabling squeezing over broad lengths.
  2. Core activation sequence: cytosolic Ca²⁺ → calmodulin → MLCK → phosphorylated myosin → force.
  3. Core relaxation sequence: falling Ca²⁺ + MLCP → dephosphorylation → reduced cycling.
  4. Receptor-specific actions: the same agonist may constrict or relax depending on its receptor.
  5. Vascular balance: NO/cGMP and local metabolites oppose Ca²⁺–MLCK-dependent constriction.

THE MOST USEFUL FIRST AID CONNECTIONS

  1. NO → guanylyl cyclase → cGMP → vascular relaxation.
  2. PDE inhibition → reduced cyclic nucleotide breakdown → prolonged relaxation signaling.
  3. L-type Ca²⁺ channel blockade → less cytosolic Ca²⁺ → less vascular contraction.
  4. α₁ versus β₂ adrenergic receptor effects depend on organ and G-protein signaling.
