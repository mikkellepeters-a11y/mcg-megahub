Palm disorders

Dupuytren contracture

Recall promptAnswer
TissueFibroproliferative palmar aponeurosis; myofibroblasts, type III collagen cords (type I normal fascia)
DigitsMost commonly ring and small; MP/PIP flexion contracture
ProgressionNodule → cord → contracture; commonly nonpainful
Risk phenotypeNorthern European ancestry association; AD variable penetrance; younger aggressive disease = diathesis
Test / thresholdPositive tabletop; lecture cites treatment for MP/PIP contracture >20°
OptionsCollagenase injection then manipulation ~36 h; fasciotomy/fasciectomy
TradeoffCollagenase faster recovery, higher recurrence than surgery (lecture)

Carpal tunnel syndrome

Recall promptAnswer
CompressionMedian nerve beneath transverse carpal ligament
Classic presentationMedian distribution paresthesias; worse at night/driving; shakes hand for relief
ExaminationPhalen, Tinel, two-point discrimination; thenar atrophy if advanced
Conservative lecture careNSAIDs, splint, early steroid injection
Surgical lecture careRelease; lecturer quotes 95% good/excellent results
FIRST AID CONNECTIONMedian palmar cutaneous branch outside tunnel → central palm spared; tunnel includes 9 flexor tendons

FIRST AID RAPID CONNECTIONS

Selective board bridges

  • Carpal tunnel → median neuropathy → spared central palm because palmar cutaneous branch runs outside tunnel.
  • Flexor sheath infection → inflammation + passive tendon stretch → pain on passive extension.
  • De Quervain → APL/EPB in first dorsal extensor compartment.
  • Palmar fascia/myofibroblasts → cords → fixed flexion contractures (Dupuytren).

Practical image recognition

RAPID-FIRE RECALL

  1. DIP cyst causing nail grooving: where is the underlying arthritis? DIP.
  2. Nail fold vs pulp abscess? Paronychia vs felon.
  3. Which digital infection should not be debrided? Herpetic whitlow.
  4. Trigger finger pulley? A1.
  5. Kanavel’s four signs? Flexed digit, fusiform swelling, sheath tenderness, passive-extension pain.
  6. Dupuytren contractile cell? Myofibroblast.
  7. Dupuytren most affected digits? 4 and 5.
  8. CTS nerve? Median.
  9. De Quervain tendons? APL and EPB.
  10. Most common dorsal ganglion origin? Scapholunate.
