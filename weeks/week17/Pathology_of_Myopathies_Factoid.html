Pathology of Myopathies — Factoid

MSK-Skin · Week 17 · Wednesday Lecture 3 · Suash Sharma, MD · Week 14 canonical rapid-review format
Anchor set: Myopathic = rounded/internal nuclei; neurogenic = angular/type grouping · DMD absent vs BMD reduced dystrophin · Ragged-red = mitochondrial · PM endomysial · DM perifascicular + rash · IBM rimmed vacuoles · Steroid/disuse = type-II atrophy.

Motor unit: where is the lesion?

  • Neuron/axon → neurogenic atrophy.
  • NMJ → myasthenia gravis (routine muscle biopsy not needed).
  • Myofiber → primary myopathy.
  • Motor unit = neuron + axon + NMJ + muscle fiber.

Normal fibers & fiber types

  • H&E: similarly sized fibers, peripheral nuclei.
  • Type I: slow, oxidative, ↑ mitochondria/lipid, endurance.
  • Type II: fast, glycolytic, ↑ glycogen, sprinting.
  • Atrophy of type II: disuse, steroid excess, alcohol.

Myopathic vs neurogenic H&E

  • Myopathy: rounded variable-size fibers; internal nuclei; necrosis/regeneration; possible fat/fibrosis.
  • Neurogenic: small angular fibers → fiber-type grouping (reinnervation) → group atrophy.
  • Degeneration: pale/necrotic/macrophages; regeneration: basophilic cytoplasm, enlarged nuclei.

Initial testing

  • Proximal weakness → often primary myopathy; distal wasting/sensory loss → consider neuropathy.
  • CK: often ↑ in myositis and early dystrophy.
  • EMG: myopathic vs neurogenic.
  • MRI STIR/T2 fat-suppressed: inflammatory muscle signal.
  • Genetic blood testing is DMD/BMD test of choice (lecture).

Dystrophy slide recognition

  • H&E: variation in fiber size + atrophy + necrosis + internal nuclei + fat/fibrosis.
  • IHC: normal continuous dystrophin rim; DMD absent rim; BMD reduced rim.
  • Western blot: dystrophin amount and size can vary in BMD.

Mitochondrial myopathy

  • Defective OXPHOS → low ATP, exercise intolerance, proximal fatigue, ophthalmoplegia.
  • May involve seizures, stroke-like episodes, hearing loss, neuropathy.
  • Ragged-red fibers on Gomori trichrome; abnormal mitochondria by EM.
  • Possible lactic acidosis; modest CK.
  • mtDNA (maternal): MELAS, MERRF, Kearns–Sayre, CPEO; nDNA: Leigh.

Dermatomyositis photos

  • Heliotrope: violaceous eyelid rash.
  • V-sign: anterior upper chest.
  • Shawl: posterior neck/shoulders.
  • Gottron papules: over finger joints; Gottron sign on extensor sites.
  • Biopsy: perivascular/perimysial infiltrate + perifascicular atrophy.
  • Adults: malignancy association; children: calcinosis.

Myositis antibodies · selective

  • DM: anti-Mi-2, TIF1, NXP2, MDA5 (lecture).
  • Antisynthetase: anti-Jo-1; ILD + arthritis + mechanic hands.
  • Necrotizing autoimmune: anti-HMGCR (statin) / anti-SRP.
  • IBM: anti-cN1A (lecture).
  • Expanded subtype details marked for familiarity only.

Type-II atrophy exam trap

  • Cause: disuse, prednisone/steroid excess, Cushing syndrome, long-term alcohol exposure.
  • Selective type-II shrinkage on ATPase typing.
  • Normal CK with weakness during steroid therapy → consider steroid myopathy over recurrent active myositis.

Neurogenic atrophy sequence

  • Loss of innervation → angular atrophic fibers.
  • Collateral sprouting → fiber-type grouping (reinnervation).
  • Chronic denervation/reinnervation → group atrophy.
  • Distinct from dystrophic rounded fibers and fibro-adipose replacement.

ALS vs SMA vs diabetes

  • ALS: UMN (spasticity, hyperreflexia, Babinski) + LMN (atrophy, fasciculations, weakness).
  • SMA: SMN1 (5q), LMN only; type 1 floppy infant, tongue fasciculations, areflexia; SMN2 copy number matters.
  • Diabetes: distal symmetric sensorimotor axonal neuropathy; glycation + sorbitol/NADPH + ischemia.

FIRST AID RAPID CONNECTIONS

  • Dystrophin deficiency → unstable sarcolemma → ↑ CK and eventual dilated cardiomyopathy.
  • MELAS → lactic acidosis + stroke-like episodes.
  • DM → malignancy association and perifascicular atrophy.
  • IBM → rimmed vacuoles + finger-flexor weakness.
  • SMA → SMN1 deficiency with SMN2 severity modifier.

RAPID-FIRE RECALL

  1. Normal nuclei? Peripheral.
  2. Type I energy? Oxidative.
  3. DMD IHC? Absent dystrophin.
  4. BMD IHC? Reduced dystrophin.
  5. Mito stain? Ragged red.
  6. PM biopsy? Endomysial.
  7. DM biopsy? Perifascicular.
  8. IBM biopsy? Rimmed vacuoles.
  9. Steroid biopsy? Type-II atrophy.
  10. Denervation histology? Angular → grouping.
