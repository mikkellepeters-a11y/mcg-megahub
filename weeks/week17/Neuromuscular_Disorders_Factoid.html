Neuromuscular Disorders — Factoid

MSK-Skin · Week 17 · Wednesday Lecture 2 · J. Ned Pruitt II, MD · Rapid-review factoid
Anchor set: P/Q Ca²⁺ channels presynaptic · nAChRs postsynaptic · EPP safety margin · MG = POSTSYNAPTIC, fatigability / rest recovery · LEMS = PRESYNAPTIC, legs>arms + areflexia + autonomic + SCLC · Botulism = vesicle fusion failure, acute descending paralysis + pupils.

Normal NMJ map

  • Active zones: P/Q-type VGCC; vesicles contain ACh.
  • Synaptic cleft basal lamina: AChE.
  • Junctional fold apex: nicotinic AChRs.
  • Fold base: voltage-gated Na⁺ channels.
  • Motor agrin → MuSK → rapsyn → nAChR clustering.

Release → EPP → contraction

  • Nerve AP → P/Q Ca²⁺ channels → ACh vesicles release → nAChR opens → EPP → muscle AP.
  • MEPP: one spontaneous ACh quantum.
  • EPP: multiple quanta evoked by nerve AP.
  • Safety margin: EPP above threshold for Na⁺-channel muscle AP.
  • Muscle AP → T tubules → SR Ca²⁺ → troponin → cross-bridges.

MG · POSTSYNAPTIC

  • AChR autoantibodies → fewer functional receptors.
  • Normal ACh release, weak/subthreshold EPP.
  • Ptosis, diplopia, dysphagia, nasal speech; may affect respiratory muscles.
  • Fatigable/diurnal weakness; better after rest.
  • Course therapy: AChE inhibitors + immunosuppression.

LEMS · PRESYNAPTIC

  • Antibodies to P/Q VGCC (handout also notes synaptotagmin).
  • ↓ nerve-terminal Ca²⁺ entry → ↓ ACh vesicle release.
  • Proximal legs > arms; difficulty rising.
  • Areflexia, dry mouth, decreased sweating; normal sensation.
  • Strong small-cell lung cancer association; may precede cancer diagnosis.

Botulism · PRESYNAPTIC

  • Clostridium botulinum toxin blocks docking/fusion → no normal ACh release.
  • Rapid cranial/bulbar weakness → descending paralysis.
  • Ophthalmoparesis, ptosis, unreactive pupils, respiratory failure.
  • Ileus, urinary retention; shared ingestion possible.
  • Infantile, ingestion, wound, cosmetic forms.
  • Course therapy: antitoxin + medical/respiratory support.

Other NMJ toxins / drugs

  • Black widow α-latrotoxin: presynaptic neurexins / Ca²⁺ influx → massive ACh release.
  • Organophosphates: irreversible AChE inhibition → prolonged ACh signaling/depolarization.
  • d-Tubocurarine: competitive nondepolarizing nAChR blockade.

FIRST AID RAPID CONNECTIONS

  • MG → thymic association and repetitive-stimulation decrement.
  • LEMS → transient facilitation after brief repeated contraction.
  • Botulism → SNARE cleavage (vesicle fusion mechanism).
  • Organophosphate → excess synaptic acetylcholine.
  • Source note: handout says AChE produces “acetyl CoA + choline”; biochemically it produces acetate + choline.

RAPID-FIRE RECALL

  1. Presynaptic active zone channel? P/Q Ca²⁺.
  2. AChR location? Fold apex.
  3. What is MEPP? One quantum.
  4. Safety margin? EPP – AP threshold.
  5. MG defect? Postsynaptic receptor.
  6. LEMS defect? Presynaptic P/Q Ca²⁺.
  7. LEMS cancer? SCLC.
  8. Botulism defect? Vesicle fusion.
  9. Black widow effect? Mass ACh release.
  10. Curare effect? Competitive nAChR blockade.
