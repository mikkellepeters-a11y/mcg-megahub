01 · Landmark anchors

MUST KNOW
Os coxae = ilium + ischium + pubis.
Ischial spine → sacrospinous ligament.
Ischial tuberosity → sacrotuberous ligament + hamstring origin.
Notches + these ligaments → sciatic foramina.
PRACTICAL
Anterior: intertrochanteric line.
Posterior: intertrochanteric crest, quadrate tubercle, gluteal tuberosity, linea aspera.
IT tract: spell out iliotibial on the practical.

DO NOT MIX THESE UP
Superior vs inferior gluteal nerves: medius/minimus/TFL vs maximus.
Sciatic vs posterior cutaneous thigh: extensive motor + sensory leg/foot supply vs cutaneous posterior thigh/buttock.
Pudendal: S2–S4, “out greater, in lesser.”

05 · Popliteal quick-map

MUST KNOW
Superolateral: biceps femoris.
Superomedial: semitendinosus + semimembranosus.
Inferolateral / inferomedial: lateral / medial gastrocnemius heads.
Superficial → deep: tibial Nerve → popliteal Vein → popliteal Artery.
HIGH YIELD
Popliteal pulse = palpate deeply with knee flexed. Small saphenous → popliteal vein. Genicular anastomoses wrap around knee. Popliteal aneurysm can cause popliteal swelling, pain and distal vascular compromise.

What ligament anchors to ischial spine?
Sacrospinous.
Which muscles stabilize the pelvis in single-leg stance?
Gluteus medius and minimus.
Which nerve lies superior to piriformis?
Superior gluteal nerve (with superior gluteal vessels).
Which thigh muscle has common fibular innervation?
Short head of biceps femoris.
Which hamstring insertion is pes anserinus?
Semitendinosus.
What does an adolescent ischial fragment suggest?
Apophyseal avulsion at the hamstring origin.
Name the superolateral popliteal boundary.
Biceps femoris.
Name the popliteal superficial-to-deep neurovascular order.
Tibial nerve → popliteal vein → popliteal artery.
What route does pudendal nerve take?
Out greater sciatic foramen, around ischial spine, in lesser sciatic foramen.
What should you write out rather than abbreviate on the practical?
Iliotibial.
