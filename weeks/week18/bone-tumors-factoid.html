Exam lens

These are retrieval cues distilled from the Bone Tumors lecture, not a second prose companion. Favor age, region, matrix, hallmark histology, and exceptions.

Diagnostic framework

  • Plain radiograph first; age + lesion location + destruction pattern + bone response + matrix.
  • Benign: often incidental, intermittently painful. Malignant: progressive rest/night pain, mass, loss of function.
  • Metastasis ≫ primary bone malignancy in older patients.

Localization shortcuts

  • Epiphysis (adult, closed growth plates) → giant cell tumor.
  • Metaphysis + adolescent → osteosarcoma; outward exostosis → osteochondroma.
  • Diaphysis + child/teen + onion skin → Ewing; diaphyseal small nidus → osteoid osteoma.
  • Pelvis/proximal femur + older adult + cartilage calcifications → chondrosarcoma.

Matrix & periosteum

  • Osteoid matrix: fluffy/cloud-like ossification. Cartilage: stippled/popcorn/arcs/rings. Fibrous dysplasia: ground glass.
  • Geographic destruction often slow; moth-eaten/permeative suggests aggressive infiltration.
  • Sunburst + Codman triangle → osteosarcoma classic; onion skin → Ewing classic.

Osteoid osteoma

  • Age 10–30; cortical long-bone nidus <2 cm, surrounding sclerosis.
  • Nocturnal pain → aspirin/NSAID response; prostaglandin-mediated.
  • Immature trabeculae rimmed by plump benign osteoblasts; ablation possible.

Osteosarcoma

  • Primary: age 10–30, metaphyseal long bones, 50% near knee; sunburst/Codman.
  • Malignant pleomorphic cells directly lay down lace-like osteoid.
  • RB1, TP53; hematogenous lung spread; chemotherapy then wide surgery.
  • Secondary: >50, Paget, radiation, infarct, fibrous dysplasia → worse prognosis.

Osteochondroma

  • Most common benign bone tumor; metaphyseal projection; marrow continuous with native bone.
  • Cartilage cap normally <2 cm; EXT1/EXT2; MHE has higher transformation risk.
  • Secondary chondrosarcoma suspicion: cap >2 cm, atypia, growth/pain.

Enchondroma — familiarity only

  • Benign medullary cartilage, hands/feet, often asymptomatic; <3 cm (course).
  • Central lytic with focal calcification; bland chondrocytes in lacunae; IDH1/IDH2.

Chondrosarcoma

  • Age 40–70, pelvis/proximal femur/humerus; painful enlarging mass.
  • Popcorn cartilage calcifications + cortical breach + soft tissue extension; lecture says no periosteal reaction.
  • Blue-gray lobules; infiltrative malignant cartilage; histologic grade predicts outcome.
  • Treatment: surgery; IDH association with enchondroma, EXT with osteochondroma.

Fibrous dysplasia

  • GNAS1 somatic GOF → arrested osteoblastic differentiation; woven bone + fibrous stroma.
  • Ground glass, expansile medullary lesion; shepherd’s crook femoral varus.
  • McCune–Albright: polyostotic FD + café-au-lait + precocious puberty.

Giant cell tumor

  • 20–40, closed physes, eccentric epiphyseal lesion; soap-bubble osteolysis, no matrix.
  • Neoplastic mononuclear stromal cells + reactive multinucleated giant cells.
  • H3F3A G34W; RANKL blockade with denosumab; locally aggressive, recurrence/rare lung spread.

Ewing sarcoma

  • Age 10–20; painful febrile diaphyseal permeative lesion ± high ESR/WBC; osteomyelitis mimic.
  • Onion-skin periosteal reaction, large soft tissue component; no malignant osteoid.
  • Small round blue glycogen-rich cells; CD99, NKX2.2; EWSR1–FLI1 t(11;22).
  • Systemic chemotherapy + surgery ± radiotherapy; metastasis worsens prognosis.

Metastatic bone disease

  • Most common malignant process in bone; older adult, vertebrae/pelvis/ribs.
  • Breast, lung, prostate, thyroid, kidney; prostate typically blastic, others often lytic/mixed.

First Aid rapid connections

  • Osteoid osteoma → prostaglandins → NSAID relief.
  • Osteosarcoma → malignant osteoid → RB1 / TP53 risk.
  • Osteochondroma → continuous marrow and EXT mutations.
  • Giant cell tumor → RANKL-active stroma → giant osteoclasts.
  • Ewing → small round blue cells → EWSR1–FLI1 fusion.
