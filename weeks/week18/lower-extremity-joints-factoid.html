FeatureMedial meniscusLateral meniscus
ShapeC-shaped, broader posteriorlyNearly circular, smaller

Planted foot: terminal extension → femur medial rotation; popliteus → femur lateral rotation → unlocks.

Cruciates intracapsular, extrasynovial. Proximal tibiofibular joint is separate.

FIRST AID RAPID CONNECTIONS

First Aid 2025 · pp. 458, 464

Inversion → ATFL (low sprain); syndesmotic injury → AITFL (high sprain). ACL/MCL injury → classic medial but more frequent lateral meniscus. Repeated kneeling → prepatellar bursitis.

Practical image recognition

RAPID-FIRE RECALL

  1. Dominant femoral head artery?Medial circumflex femoral.
  2. Posterior hip dislocation limb position?Shortened, medially rotated.
  3. Valgus stress tests?MCL.
  4. Lachman tests?ACL.
  5. Classic vs actual triad?Medial vs lateral meniscus with ACL/MCL.
  6. Popliteus planted-foot action?Lateral rotation of femur.
  7. Most stable ankle position?Dorsiflexion.
  8. Most common inversion sprain?ATFL.
  9. Medial arch keystone?Talar head.
  10. Spring ligament failure?Pes planus.
