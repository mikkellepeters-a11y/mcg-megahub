FIRST AID RAPID CONNECTIONS

Selective high-yield bridges

Compartment syndrome → pressure impairs perfusion; pulses can remain palpable · Virchow triad → stasis + endothelial injury + hypercoagulability → DVT/PE · Common fibular injury → foot drop + weak eversion · L5 radiculopathy may also cause foot drop but can weaken inversion via tibialis posterior · Tarsal tunnel → tibial nerve compression → plantar sensory symptoms.

Practical image recognition

RAPID-FIRE RECALL

  1. Anterior compartment nerve? Deep fibular
  2. Lateral compartment nerve? Superficial fibular
  3. First web-space innervation? Deep fibular
  4. Common fibular injury site? Fibular neck
  5. Tarsal tunnel order? TP–FDL–artery–nerve–FHL
  6. Posterior ankle pulse? Posterior tibial
  7. DP pulse location? Lateral to EHL
  8. Unlocks knee? Popliteus
  9. Soleus test position? Knee flexed 90°
  10. Nerve to interossei? Lateral plantar
