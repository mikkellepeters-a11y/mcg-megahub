Exercise is Medicine — Factoid Sheet

MSK-Skin · Week 18 · Thursday Lecture 1 · C. Ledford · Course-first rapid review
Last-minute framework: assess PA → prescribe FITT–VP → choose low-impact aerobic + resistance + flexibility for OA → screen for symptoms and disease → progress gradually.

Definitions & intensity

  • PA: skeletal muscle movement ↑ energy expenditure above rest.
  • Exercise: planned, structured, repetitive PA to maintain/improve fitness.
  • Fitness: attributes enabling PA.
  • METs: light <3; moderate 3–<6; vigorous >6 on slide 10 (slide 42 uses ≥6).
  • Moderate: talk, not sing; vigorous: difficult to talk.

Prevention & mortality

  • Physical inactivity = fourth risk on historical WHO chart; behavior = 40% of premature deaths in cited model.
  • Georgia: 27.9% no leisure activity; 49.1% met aerobic target (2019).
  • Largest mortality difference between least-fit and next fitness group.
  • 50 MET-h/wk ↑ associated with 26% ↓ CHD; running 42%, brisk walking 18% lower risk in cited study.
  • Exercise-associated lipid changes independent of AHA diet adherence.

Exercise vs drugs & cancer

  • Network meta-analysis: 16 meta-analyses; 305 studies; 339,274 patients.
  • CHD, stroke, HF, prediabetes; stroke exercise OR 0.09 (0.01–0.72). Do not infer universal drug replacement.
  • Strong evidence: breast 12–21% and colon 19% lower incidence; strong dose-response.
  • Also bladder, endometrial, esophageal adenocarcinoma, gastric, renal, lung.
  • Cancer-related fatigue → aerobic activity emphasized.

Physician role & PAVS

  • Assess → advise/write Rx → refer.
  • Written advice + counseling: sustained 6-month effect; associated with 25% lower mortality at cited activity difference.
  • PAVS = days/wk × minutes/day.
  • 150 min moderate OR 75 min vigorous; 1 vigorous min = 2 moderate min.
  • Motivational interviewing, goals, problem solving, self-monitoring; sit less/move more.

OA: treatment evidence

  • 2015 Cochrane: pain/function benefit comparable to NSAID effect immediately and 2–6 mo afterward.
  • Aerobic + strengthening; early intervention favored.
  • Group = home effectiveness; patient preference and adherence matter.
  • Category 1A = meta-analysis of RCTs; 1B = ≥1 RCT; recommendation A directly category 1.
  • Individualize to age, mobility, comorbidity.

FITT–VP: frequency, intensity, time

  • F: aerobic 3–5 d/wk; resistance 2–3 nonconsecutive d/wk; flexibility daily.
  • I: 40–60% age-predicted max HR. Sedentary range conflicts: slide 33 50–60%; slide 37 30–40%.
  • 220 − age; age 55 × 60% of predicted max = 99 bpm (165 × .60).
  • T: 150 min moderate or 75 vigorous weekly; 10-min bouts listed; 5 min if needed; general PAVS permits any duration.

FITT–VP: type, volume, progression

  • Type: low-impact walk/cycle/pool; all major muscle groups, concentric/eccentric, isometric if pain limits motion; flexibility in warm-up/cool-down.
  • V: MET-min/wk.
  • P: OA aerobic +5–10 min/wk; resistance +10%/wk (reps OR weight).
  • Pain persists 2 h → alter activity. General progression: +5–10 min every 1–2 wk for 4–6 wk.

Practical OA advice

  • Shoes: 300–500 miles or 300 hours; at least annually (lecture).
  • Pool: 83–88°F → relaxation, ↓ pain.
  • Time activity at peak analgesic effect if already using medication.
  • Isometric = tension without appreciable joint motion (clinical enrichment).

Safety: acute vs habitual exercise

  • Sudden vigorous exertion transiently ↑ AMI risk; spike greatest in sedentary people.
  • Regular exercise reduces relative triggering risk.
  • Screen regular exercise → known CV/metabolic/renal disease → symptoms.
  • Concerning symptoms override known disease status (see table).

Comorbidities

  • HIV: aerobic activity not harmful to immune function/progression in lecture; physician clearance recommended due to treatment-associated CV risk.
  • Cancer: exercise for pain/fatigue; aerobic best emphasized for fatigue; before treatment or survivorship.
  • Depression: yoga mono/adjunct; anxiety: adjunct yoga (panic); mindfulness adjunct; PA associated with lower depression risk.

FIRST AID RAPID CONNECTIONS

OA → mechanical degeneration

  • Cartilage damage → pain after use, asymmetric narrowing, osteophytes, subchondral sclerosis/cysts. (p. 472)
  • RA → autoimmune pannus, symmetric narrowing, erosions, long morning stiffness.

Exercise → cardiovascular physiology

  • CO = HR × SV; ↑ contractility and SV early, HR drives further increase as SV plateaus; ↓ TPR. (pp. 289–291)

NSAIDs → analgesia and risk

  • COX inhibition ↓ prostaglandins; GI ulcer and renal ischemia risk. (p. 496)
