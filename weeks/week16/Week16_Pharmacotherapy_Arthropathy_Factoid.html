01 · NSAIDs and analgesia

Drug → effect → caution

Drug/classTarget or featureCritical clue
AspirinIrreversible COX; TXA₂ suppressionPlatelet effect lasts ~8–10 days; bleeding, GI and renal toxicity
IbuprofenReversible COX inhibitionMay interfere with antiplatelet aspirin; ~2 h half-life
NaproxenCOX-1/2 inhibitionLithium, BP medication and anticoagulant interactions
IndomethacinPotent NSAIDGout; also PDA closure and Bartter syndrome in course
Meloxicam / etodolac / diclofenacPreferential COX-2 activity (slide terminology)Still consider NSAID class risks
AcetaminophenCentral COX-related actions in low-peroxide environmentAnalgesic/antipyretic; not strong anti-inflammatory; hepatotoxic overdose

Prostaglandin logic

  • COX-2: inflammatory PGs → nociceptor sensitization → pain, swelling; inhibition gives analgesic/anti-inflammatory benefit.
  • COX-1: gastric mucosal PG protection and platelet TXA₂ → inhibition explains ulcers and bleeding.
  • Antipyresis: reduce hypothalamic PG signaling downstream of inflammatory cytokines.
  • Low-dose aspirin: 40–80 mg/day in Barman slides; moderate 300–2400 mg/day analgesia; high 2400–4000 mg/day anti-inflammatory (course dose categories).
  • Salicylate overdose: urine alkalinization with sodium bicarbonate increases renal excretion.

03 · Gout: flare vs urate prevention

Acute flare logic

  • Professor case: acute red, swollen monoarticular knee with risk factors and imaging showing OA features → do not assume OA explains an acute hot joint.
  • Course MCQ: indomethacin selected over acetaminophen/aspirin/morphine for acute inflammatory gout; colchicine is also taught as effective early anti-gout treatment.
  • Colchicine: tubulin binding → ↓ granulocyte motility; GI distress and marrow toxicity.
  • Safety: septic arthritis is in the explicit monoarthritis differential; joint fluid analysis is needed when infection cannot be excluded (BOARD / CLINICAL ENRICHMENT).

05 · FIRST AID RAPID CONNECTIONS

Lecture → Step 1 bridges

  • Aspirin → irreversible platelet COX-1 → reduced TXA₂ and prolonged bleeding tendency.
  • NSAIDs → impaired renal PG-mediated perfusion → increased AKI risk with renal compromise.
  • Methotrexate → folate-related toxicity + adenosine-mediated anti-inflammatory effects; distinguish from leflunomide.
  • TNF inhibition → impaired granuloma maintenance → latent tuberculosis reactivation risk.
  • Allopurinol → xanthine oxidase inhibition → prevents urate accumulation; interaction with azathioprine is a classic board association.
  • Colchicine → microtubule inhibition → decreased neutrophil chemotaxis.

Selective First Aid / board connections; drug mechanisms and safety concepts primarily originate in the supplied lecture materials.
