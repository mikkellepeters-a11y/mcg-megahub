Pain patterns

  • Acute: shorter course, cause often identifiable → treat cause + relief.
  • Chronic: persists months–years; focus on function, sleep, mood and pain control.
  • Do not infer pain from vitals, appearance or sleep.
  • Pathway: tissue → DRG → dorsal horn → STT → thalamus → cortex.

Local anesthesia & α₂

  • Local anesthetics: voltage-gated Na⁺ blockade → no impulse propagation.
  • Short: procaine, chloroprocaine. Intermediate: tetracaine, mepivacaine, lidocaine. Long: bupivacaine, ropivacaine.
  • Bupivacaine: major toxicity risk. Watch tinnitus/seizures/arrhythmias.
  • α₂ agonists: clonidine, dexmedetomidine; hypotension, bradycardia (Dex).

Neuropathic treatment

  • TCAs: amitriptyline, nortriptyline, desipramine → monoamine reuptake inhibition; anticholinergic effects.
  • SNRIs: duloxetine, venlafaxine; venlafaxine less ideal in HTN.
  • Pregabalin/gabapentin: α₂δ calcium-channel subunit → ↓ transmitter release; dizziness.
  • Topical capsaicin: TRPV1 stimulation → defunctionalization; initially burns.

04 · Cases and treatment selection

WHO three-step ladder

  • Step 1: nonopioid ± adjuvant for mild pain.
  • Step 2: weak opioid for persisting/moderate pain.
  • Step 3: strong opioid for severe pain.
  • Acute severe pain: may require immediate higher-step therapy.
  • Case A: inflammatory back pain + iritis (AS) → ibuprofen.
  • Case B: severe sickle-cell crisis despite home analgesia → morphine.

Safety & function

  • NSAIDs: GI ulcer/bleeding, renal injury, CV risk; relative selectivity matters.
  • Local anesthetic: CNS and CV toxicity if systemically absorbed.
  • Opioid: consider sedation/respiratory depression (board enrichment).
  • Non-drug: massage, manipulation, acupuncture, TENS, spinal stimulation.

First Aid rapid connections
