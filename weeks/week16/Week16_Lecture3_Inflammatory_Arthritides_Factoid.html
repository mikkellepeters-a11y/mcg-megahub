Inflammatory vs mechanical back pain

  • Inflammatory: onset <45, >1h morning stiffness, improves with activity, rest worsens, night pain.
  • Mechanical: rest improves, activity often worsens, brief morning stiffness.
  • Alternating buttock pain + good NSAID response → consider axial SpA.

Five shared SpA clues

  • 1 Inflammatory axial back pain / sacroiliitis.
  • 2 Peripheral arthritis.
  • 3 Dactylitis = sausage digit.
  • 4 Enthesitis = Achilles/plantar fascia etc.
  • 5 Acute anterior uveitis.

HLA-B27 · cytokines · new bone

  • Environmental factors + HLA-B27 / other loci.
  • Enthesitis at tendon/ligament insertion.
  • IL-23/T cells → TNF-α / IL-17 → inflammation and erosion.
  • IL-22 → new bone → syndesmophytes / ankylosis.

Imaging: SI joint and spine

  • First: SI radiograph.
  • If unrevealing: SI STIR/T2 fat-suppressed MRI for marrow edema.
  • SI X-ray: pseudo-widening/erosions → sclerosis/narrowing → fusion.
  • Bamboo spine: thin, continuous marginal syndesmophytes.

Ankylosing spondylitis

  • Inflammatory axial pain; symmetric sacroiliitis.
  • Aortitis / aortic regurgitation.
  • Costovertebral ankylosis → reduced chest excursion.
  • Apical pulmonary fibrosis.

IBD-associated peripheral arthritis

  • Type 1 = asymmetric large-joint oligoarthritis parallels IBD activity.
  • Type 2 = symmetric MCP polyarthritis independent of IBD activity.
  • Erythema nodosum = painful shin nodules.
  • Pyoderma gangrenosum = undermined violaceous ulcer.

Reactive arthritis

  • Sterile arthritis ≤4 weeks after GI/GU infection.
  • GI: Salmonella, Shigella, Yersinia, Campylobacter; GU: Chlamydia.
  • Asymmetric lower limb oligoarthritis; conjunctivitis ± urethritis.
  • Full conjunctivitis–urethritis–arthritis triad in ~1/3.
  • Keratoderma blennorrhagicum / circinate balanitis.

Psoriatic arthritis

  • Psoriasis often precedes arthritis; nail pitting/onycholysis.
  • DIP disease, dactylitis, asymmetric oligoarthritis.
  • Arthritis mutilans → telescoping digits.
  • X-ray: erosions + periosteal new bone; late pencil-in-cup.

Both crystal diseases · mechanism

  • Deposited crystal → NLRP3 inflammasome → caspase-1 → active IL-1β → hot swollen joint.
  • Acute treatment shared: NSAIDs, glucocorticoids, colchicine, IL-1 antagonist anakinra.
  • Colchicine impairs microtubule assembly.

Gout: risk and prevention

  • Hyperuricemia strongest risk; underexcretion ~90%, overproduction ~10%.
  • Precipitating factors: surgery, dehydration, thiazides, renal decline, dietary excess.
  • Urate lowering: recurrent flares, tophus, radiographic damage, selected high-risk first flare.
  • Allopurinol first line if no contraindication; goal urate <6.
  • Anti-inflammatory prophylaxis during urate mobilization.

CPPD: characteristic contrasts

  • Older adults; associations: hemochromatosis, hyperparathyroidism, hypomagnesemia, hypophosphatasia.
  • Acute monoarthritis classically knee/wrist.
  • No therapy removes deposited CPP crystals.
  • Manage metabolic contributors; colchicine may prevent recurrence.

FIRST AID RAPID CONNECTIONS

  • MSU crystal → NLRP3 → IL-1β explains acute gout flare.
  • Allopurinol/febuxostat inhibit xanthine oxidase for urate reduction.
  • Urate crystals = strong negative; calcium pyrophosphate = weak positive.
  • HLA-B27 strengthens the SpA association but does not alone diagnose disease.

RAPID-FIRE RECALL

  • Back pain relieved by activity? Inflammatory.
  • Enthesitis site? Tendon/ligament attachment.
  • SI imaging after normal X-ray? STIR MRI.
  • Jug-handle syndesmophytes? PsA/reactive.
  • IBD Type 1 activity? Parallels bowel activity.
  • Post-Chlamydia sterile arthritis? Reactive.
  • PsA late hand deformity? Pencil-in-cup.
  • Needle/negative crystal? Gout.
  • CPPD key X-ray clue? Chondrocalcinosis.
  • Gout urate goal? <6 mg/dL.
