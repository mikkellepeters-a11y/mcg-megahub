Alternative Pain Control — Factoid

MSK–Skin · Week 16 · Holistic Patient Care · Dahl-Smith · Canonical Week 14 factoid layout
Anchor set: Complementary WITH conventional vs alternative INSTEAD · Nociceptive / nociplastic / neuropathic · peripheral threshold vs central amplification · hyperalgesia vs allodynia · Mediterranean pattern · patient-centered risk assessment · NCCIH / Cochrane / About Herbs.

CAM language

  • Complementary: combined with conventional care.
  • Alternative: replaces conventional treatment.
  • Integrative: safe, effective conventional + CAM approaches with whole-person emphasis.

NIH CAM taxonomy

  • Biologically based: botanicals/supplements.
  • Mind–body: meditation, imagery, biofeedback.
  • Manual: massage, osteopathy, chiropractic.
  • Energy therapies; whole medical systems (TCM, Ayurveda, homeopathy, etc.).

CAM prevalence

  • 2002 19.2% → 2022 36.7% (selected adult modalities, NCCIH chart).
  • Lecture quiz answer rounded to 36%.
  • Use for pain ≠ overall use denominator.

Three mechanisms of pain

  • Nociceptive: tissue injury/activation; e.g., NSAIDs, interventions.
  • Nociplastic: altered processing/central sensitization; TCAs/SNRIs discussed.
  • Neuropathic: nerve lesion/dysfunction; pregabalin/gabapentin discussed.

Stress → pain pathways

  • Hypothalamus CRH/CRF → HPA/sympathetic response.
  • Injury → mast cell mediators → dorsal horn.
  • Key mediators: substance P, glutamate, CGRP.
  • Amygdala: threat/fear; hippocampus: safety/context.

Sensitization: differentiate

  • Peripheral: inflammatory threshold lowering.
  • Central: amplified ascending signaling/reduced inhibition in lecture model.
  • Hyperalgesia: increased pain to painful stimulus.
  • Allodynia: pain from normally nonpainful stimulus.

Protective domains

  • Psychological: self-efficacy, coping.
  • Lifestyle: physical activity, sleep, diet.
  • Social: support and resources.
  • Physical: strength, walking, ROM.

Mediterranean diet

  • Vegetables, fruits, legumes, whole grains, fish, unsaturated fats.
  • Lecture: associated with OA pain, stiffness, QOL benefits.
  • Do not favor higher omega-6:omega-3 ratio in slide quiz.

Fat pathway distinction

  • MUFA → omega-9 / oleic acid.
  • Omega-6 → arachidonic acid-derived mediators; omega-3 → EPA/DHA pathways.
  • Source discrepancy: slide calls GLA both anti-inflammatory and inflammatory in different bullets.

Topicals and supplements

  • Diclofenac 1% = topical NSAID; topical lidocaine = local analgesic.
  • Turmeric/curcumin (+ piperine), magnesium glycinate, SAM-e discussed.
  • SAM-e caution: bipolar disorder.
  • GMP/USP/NSF labels ≠ proof of efficacy.

Off-label / uncertain

  • Low-dose naltrexone: slide proposes glial/cytokine modulation; limited/emerging application.
  • Ketamine: NMDA antagonist; lecture cautions limited evidence in reviewed pain contexts.
  • Do not universalize slide percentages to all patients.

Movement and mindfulness

  • Exercise A; massage B; OMT A/B in lecture rating table.
  • Yoga A/B; CBT B; meditation/breathing/imagery A; tai chi/qigong A.
  • Mindfulness meta-analysis: pain/anxiety/depression benefit; disability not clearly changed.

Acupuncture

  • Acupuncture A/B; safety A; dry needle/trigger-point B (lecture ratings).
  • Proposed opioidergic, monoamine and GABA pathways.
  • Battlefield auricular acupuncture is a distinct technique.

Tai chi vs qigong

  • Tai chi: martial arts-derived sequences of movement.
  • Qigong: generally simpler/stationary poses with breath/movement emphasis.
  • Traditional qi framework ≠ established biomedical mechanism.

Counseling script

  • Ask about all products/practices.
  • Clarify goals, assess condition-specific evidence and safety.
  • Check interactions, clinician credentials, cost, replacement of indicated care.
  • Agree on measurable outcomes and follow-up.

Trusted resources

  • NIH NCCIH; Cochrane.
  • MedlinePlus supplements.
  • Memorial Sloan Kettering About Herbs.
  • VA Whole Health, UW Integrative Health, NatMed.
