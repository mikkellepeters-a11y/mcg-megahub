Non-Inflammatory Arthritis — Factoid

MSK-Skin · Week 16 · Lecture 2 · Danaher · Week 14 canonical rapid-review layout
Anchor set: Cartilage = type II collagen · Trauma / malalignment / genetic susceptibility → OA · OA X-ray = narrowing + osteophytes + sclerosis + cysts · OCD = cartilage AND subchondral bone · Stable juvenile OCD may be observed; unstable lesions → surgery.

Cartilage: 20-second foundation

  • Hyaline articular cartilage → type II collagen.
  • Distributes forces and minimizes joint friction.
  • Cartilage injury can initiate degenerative change.

Three drivers of degeneration

  • Trauma: fracture/ligament-associated injury or isolated cartilage trauma.
  • Malalignment: varus, valgus or rotation → uneven load.
  • Genetics: rare skeletal/joint malformations.

OA structural signature

  • Joint-space narrowing = cartilage loss.
  • Osteophytes = marginal bony overgrowth.
  • Sclerosis = denser subchondral bone.
  • Subchondral cysts = bony cystic changes.

OA hand recognition

  • Heberden nodes → DIP.
  • Bouchard nodes → PIP.
  • Chronic mechanical change ≠ primary inflammatory synovitis.

Osteochondritis dissecans: essentials

  • Cartilage + subchondral bone lesion.
  • Likely reduced regional vascularity + repetitive mechanical stress.
  • Not inflammation, despite name.
  • Juvenile onset ~10–15 years with open physes.

OCD presentation

  • Insidious, progressively increasing activity pain.
  • Often hard to localize; subjective mechanical symptoms.
  • Exam may be deceptively benign; +/- effusion or stiffness.
  • Characteristic X-ray; early MRI for further assessment.

Acute cartilage defect

  • Traumatic event; no antecedent pain.
  • Sudden focal pain and swelling/effusion.
  • X-ray may be normal.
  • Compare with more indolent OCD.

OCD care: stability first

  • Stable juvenile, open physes → protected/non-WB, bracing, reassess.
  • Stable asymptomatic adult may be observed.
  • Unstable lesion → consider surgery.

Surgery: name the option

  • Microfracture/debridement: marrow-stimulation repair.
  • Fixation: screws, darts, bone pegs, K-wires.
  • OATS: autograft, lecture <2–3 cm.
  • OCA: allograft, lecture >2–3 cm.

OA treatment ladder

  • First: activity modification, PT, weight loss, NSAIDs if appropriate.
  • Second: selected injections (lecture includes steroid and viscosupplementation).
  • Third: surgery when indicated.

Image selection: X-ray first

  • Young patient + effusion, trauma, locking, reduced ROM, or year-round sport-related joint pain → X-ray.
  • Concern for OCD/occult injury → consider early MRI.
  • OA without mechanical locking → do not routinely order MRI (lecture emphasis).

Imaging + injury traps

  • Positive X-ray or intra-articular fracture/effusion in a young patient warrants more detailed evaluation.
  • CT entails ionizing radiation; use sparingly in young patients.
  • Do not equate normal radiograph with normal cartilage.

DON’T MIX THESE UP

  • OCD: insidious, osteochondral, often normal exam.
  • Traumatic defect: immediate pain/effusion, X-ray may be normal.
  • OA: chronic degenerative joint-space and bone changes.

First Aid rapid connections

  • Type II collagen ↔ articular hyaline cartilage.
  • Cartilage degeneration → narrowed joint space.
  • Reactive bone → sclerosis and osteophytes.
  • Mechanical loading → compartment-specific OA.
